As soon as the year turns, the question that pops up in family group chats is, "Which months are good next year?" For many Javanese families, the answer doesn't start from the Gregorian calendar but from the Javanese one. The month comes first, then its Gregorian dates.

The trouble is that Javanese months shift against the Gregorian calendar every year, so this year's Besar does not fall in the same month as next year's. This article sums up the months considered good and those usually avoided, then maps every Javanese month to its Gregorian dates through 2027.

## The Javanese and Hijri calendars

The Javanese calendar in use today follows the moon, like the Hijri calendar, and its month names pair up with the Hijri ones: Sura with Muharram, Pasa with Ramadan, Besar with Dhul Hijjah, and so on. That is why a Javanese year is about eleven days shorter than a Gregorian one, and its months keep moving forward every year.

Even so, the months don't always start on the same day. The Indonesian Hijri Calendar published by the Ministry of Religious Affairs lists the Javanese date in a column of its own, and in some months the Javanese month begins a day after the Hijri one. Communities that follow the Aboge count may differ further still. The table in this article uses the Javanese column of the ministry's calendar.

As for the year, January to early June 2027 still falls in Javanese year 1960, a Be year. Year 1961, a Wawu year, begins on 1 Sura, which falls on 6 June 2027.

## Months considered good and months avoided

This list comes from primbon books and from customs many families still keep. The sources don't always agree, so treat it as a general picture.

**Considered good:** Jumadilakir, Rejeb, Ruwah and Besar. These four appear on almost every list, including those quoting the Primbon Lengkap by Tjakraningrat.

**Usually avoided:** Sura, Pasa, Sela (also called Apit or Dulkangidah) and Mulud. Sura is honoured as a sacred month and kept more for quiet reflection. Pasa is the fasting month. Sela, the month wedged between two religious holidays, is explained in one study in Gresik by the phrase "diseseli barang olo", slipped in with something bad.

**Allowed with conditions:** some primbon say Sapar, Mulud, Jumadilawal and Sawal can still be used if the month contains both a Tuesday Kliwon and a Friday Kliwon. Mulud appears on two lists at once, which shows how much families' rules vary.

One more note: in Islamic teaching no month is bad for marrying. Many Javanese Muslim families hold both at once, choosing a month considered good by custom without treating other months as religiously forbidden. The government's dates for Ramadan, Shawwal and Dhul Hijjah still await the official isbat session, so they can shift by a day. If your chosen date sits right at the edge of one of those months, check again after the official announcement.

## Good-month windows in 2027

From the table above, there are five windows Javanese families look for most:

1. **Early in the year, Rejeb and Ruwah: 11 December 2026 to 7 February 2027.** Two good months back to back, suited to couples who want to marry before the fasting month. The New Year and Chinese New Year long weekends fall in this span.
2. **Besar: 8 May to 5 June 2027.** The most popular window of mid-year. Iduladha falls on 17 May, with a long break from Saturday 15 May to Thursday 20 May thanks to the collective leave and Vesak nearby.
3. **Jumadilakir: 1 to 29 November 2027.** The only good month in the second half of the year before Rejeb.
4. **Rejeb 1961: 30 November to 29 December 2027.** It runs straight on from Jumadilakir, making November to late December one long stretch considered good.
5. **Ruwah 1961: from 30 December 2027**, continuing into early 2028.

Because many families target the same windows, weekends in Besar and through November and December tend to sell out quickly at popular venues. If you are aiming for one of them, start looking for a venue as early as you can.

## Public holidays in 2027 worth noting

Public holidays and collective leave decide whether guests can come, whether vendors are free and how busy the roads are. These are the ones that matter most, according to the joint ministerial decree on 2027 public holidays and collective leave:

- **Ramadan** is expected from 8 February to 9 March. Receptions are rarely held in this month.
- **Nyepi** on Monday 8 March, followed by **Idulfitri** on 10 and 11 March, with collective leave on 9, 12 and 15 March. From Saturday 6 March to Monday 15 March is effectively one long break, and many people travel home.
- **Iduladha** on Monday 17 May, with collective leave on 18 May and **Vesak** on 20 May.
- **1 Muharram (1 Sura)** on Sunday 6 June.
- **The Prophet's Birthday** on Sunday 15 August, and **Independence Day** on Tuesday 17 August.
- **Isra Mi'raj** falls twice in 2027: on 5 January and 26 December. That is not a misprint; the Hijri year is shorter than the Gregorian one.

Collective leave is not mandatory for private employers, so don't assume all your guests will be off on those dates.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 1 Sura the same as 1 Muharram?

They are counterparts, but the dates can differ by a day. In 2027 both fall on 6 June according to the ministry's calendar, while in 2026 1 Muharram fell on 16 June and 1 Sura on 17 June.

### Which month is safest if the families don't agree?

Besar and Rejeb are the two months that appear on almost every list of good months, whatever the primbon. If two families have different rules, one of these two can usually be accepted by everyone.

### Is marrying in the month of Sura forbidden?

By custom, Sura is generally avoided for celebrations, though not everyone sees it that way. In Islamic teaching there is no prohibition on marrying in any month. In the end it comes down to what the families agree.

### Why do dates in other articles differ by a day?

There are three possibilities: the source uses the Hijri date rather than the Javanese column; the source follows the Aboge count; or the dates for Ramadan, Shawwal and Dhul Hijjah have been adjusted after the isbat session. For the final decision, check with the elders in your family.
