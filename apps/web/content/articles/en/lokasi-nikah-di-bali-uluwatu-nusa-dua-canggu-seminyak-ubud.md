The first photo that made you fall for Bali was probably taken on an Uluwatu clifftop at sunset. The second, which your best friend sent last week, shows a long table in the middle of Ubud's rice fields. Both are beautiful, but they're a long way apart, and choosing between them decides a lot of other things: how long your guests sit in a car after landing, where they sleep, and what happens if it rains in January.

This article compares the five areas couples search for most: Uluwatu, Nusa Dua, Canggu, Seminyak and Ubud. We've deliberately left prices out. Cost depends more on the type of venue and the guest count than on the area, and you'll find typical figures on the cost page we link at the end.

## Start with your guests, not the photos

Before you open a single venue list, answer two questions. First, how many guests are flying in, and how many nights will they stay? A wedding for 30 people who all stay three nights needs something very different from a reception for 200 guests who mostly fly in and out on the same weekend.

Second, will the ceremony and reception be in the same place? If not, the travel time between them counts too, and in Bali 10 km can take a long time.

## The five areas at a glance

The travel times below are estimates from I Gusti Ngurah Rai Airport, taken from Kiwitaxi's transfer guide. They can be much longer at peak hours, and other sources give different figures. Holiday Extras, for example, lists about 55 minutes to Nusa Dua and almost two hours to Ubud. Treat them as a rough guide, not a timetable.

| Area | Setting | From the airport (approx.) | In the wet season |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uluwatu | Limestone cliffs on the south-west tip of the Bukit Peninsula, facing the Indian Ocean | 23 km, 45 to 70 minutes | Clifftop ceremony spots are exposed to wind and rain |
| Nusa Dua | A gated resort enclave on the east side of the Bukit Peninsula, white-sand beach | 13 km, 20 to 40 minutes | The widest choice of covered event spaces |
| Canggu | Villas, beach clubs and what's left of the rice fields on the south-west coast | 20 km, 1 to 1.5 hours | Villas don't always have covered space for every guest |
| Seminyak | Villas, restaurants and hotels, running into Kuta and Legian | 10 km, 30 to 45 minutes | The surf can get big in bad weather |
| Ubud | Valleys, rice fields and forest inland in Gianyar | about 38 km, 1.5 to 2 hours | The wettest of the five |

## Uluwatu: cliffs and sunsets

Uluwatu sits on the south-west tip of the Bukit Peninsula, within the village of Pecatu. The cliffs are limestone lifted from the seabed, and on one of the clifftops stands Pura Luhur Uluwatu, one of Bali's nine directional temples. Today the cliffs are lined with high-end resorts, boutique hotels and private villas, and many have a lawn or deck for an ocean-facing ceremony.

The appeal is obvious: open ocean and the sun setting behind you. The drawbacks are access and weather. The roads are narrow, ride-hailing is restricted in parts of the area, and guests staying elsewhere will need a shuttle. In the wet season the clifftops catch the wind, so ask the venue where the ceremony moves if it rains, and whether the backup spot still has a view.

## Nusa Dua: big resorts and an easy plan B

Nusa Dua is an integrated resort area of about 350 hectares on the east side of the Bukit Peninsula, managed by ITDC, the Indonesia Tourism Development Corporation. The core is gated, with security checkpoints at the entrances, and holds more than twenty resorts, most of them five-star. For couples with a lot of guests it's the simplest choice: guests can stay in the same resort or the one next door, walk to the reception, and never depend on traffic.

In the wet season Nusa Dua is also the least stressful. Its big resorts generally have indoor function rooms, so the backup plan doesn't have to be improvised. If you're picturing an indoor reception from the start, we've reviewed [hotel ballrooms in Bali](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/wedding-venue/10-ballroom-hotel-paling-glamour-di-bali-buat-resepsi-wedding-yang-bikin-tamu-speechless).

## Canggu: villas, beach clubs and traffic

Canggu is in the North Kuta district, about 10 km north-west of Kuta. Since the early 2020s it has become one of Bali's most popular destinations, and villas and hotels have replaced much of its rice fields and open land. A Canggu wedding is usually held at a private villa or a beach club, with a more relaxed feel than a resort.

What you need to plan around is traffic. Jalan Raya Canggu, the main road, is notorious for jams, and ride-hailing is restricted in parts of the area. If your guests are spread across several villas, set up a shuttle schedule early. In the wet season, make sure the villa has enough covered space for everyone, because villas rarely have a room the size of a ballroom.

## Seminyak: close, lively and full of places for dinner

Seminyak was once a separate village and has now merged with Kuta and Legian on the west coast. Its streets are packed with restaurants, bars, shops and villas, plus luxury spas and hotels. For a wedding, Seminyak suits couples who want guests to be able to walk to dinner afterwards, or who are planning a welcome dinner and a brunch in the days around the wedding.

It's one of the closest areas to the airport, but Seminyak's roads also clog at peak hours. For a beach ceremony in the wet season, remember that the sea off southern Bali can get rough. In a late-October 2026 warning, BMKG forecast waves of up to three metres in the waters south of Bali.

## Ubud: valleys, rice fields and wedding packages in the hills

Ubud is a town in Gianyar Regency known as the centre of Balinese arts and culture. Around it are rice fields, plantations and river valleys, and wedding packages at the resorts around Ubud are a common search for couples from overseas. It feels the most different of the five: no ocean, but green everywhere you look.

Ubud is also the furthest from the airport and the wettest. Copernicus climate model data for 1991 to 2020 puts Ubud's annual rainfall at around 2,400 mm, while the climate normals for the Ngurah Rai station near the airport come to only about 1,800 mm. If you're marrying in Ubud between December and February, a covered backup plan is non-negotiable. If you hold the main event on the coast, Ubud still makes a good day trip for guests a day or two afterwards.

If your priority is guests who fly in and out on the same weekend, the area next to the airport is worth a look. ## The wet season and your wedding date

According to BMKG, Indonesia's weather agency, the 2026/2027 wet season starts in parts of Bali in November 2026 and across most of the island in December 2026, peaking in January and February 2027. Whichever area you choose, ask the venue three things before paying a deposit: where the backup plan is, how many people it holds, and when the call to move there gets made.

Your date also needs checking against the Balinese calendar. Nyepi, the Balinese Day of Silence, falls on Monday 8 March 2027. On Nyepi, Ngurah Rai Airport stops operating for 24 hours, as it did for Nyepi 2026, and other Balinese holy days can affect vendor availability. ## Frequently asked questions

### Which area is closest to the airport?

Kuta and Tuban sit right next to the airport. Of the five areas covered here, Seminyak and Nusa Dua are the closest.

### Which area is safest for a wet-season wedding?

Nusa Dua, because its big resorts generally have indoor function rooms. Ubud is the wettest.

### Can we hold the ceremony in one area and the reception in another?

Yes, but time the drive at the same hour as your event, not when the roads are empty, and arrange transport for every guest.
