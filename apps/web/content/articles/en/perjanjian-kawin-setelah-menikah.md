Many couples only start thinking about a marital agreement years after the wedding. The trigger is usually concrete: a property notary asks about the status of your assets, one of you starts borrowing for a business, parents are about to pass on land, or a foreign spouse wants the Indonesian partner to be able to buy a home in their own name.

Before 2016 the answer was simple and disappointing: too late. Not any more. This article explains how a marital agreement made during the marriage works, often called a postnup or *postnuptial agreement*. If you are not married yet, start with our [complete guide to prenuptial agreements](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tips-hubungan/perjanjian-pranikah-panduan-lengkap).

## The legal basis: Constitutional Court Decision 69/PUU-XIII/2015

Article 29 of Law No. 1 of 1974 on Marriage originally allowed an agreement only "at the time of or before the marriage takes place". Constitutional Court Decision No. 69/PUU-XIII/2015, read on 27 October 2016, changed its meaning. Under that decision, an agreement may be made "at the time of, before, or during the marriage", by mutual consent, and ratified by the marriage registrar or a notary.

The case was brought by Ike Farida, an Indonesian married to a Japanese national, who could not buy an apartment because she had no separation-of-property agreement. That is why mixed-nationality couples use the decision most, but it applies to every couple.

## When the agreement takes effect

This is the part most often misunderstood. The same decision reinterpreted Article 29(3) to read: the agreement "takes effect from the time the marriage takes place, unless otherwise specified in the Marriage Agreement."

In other words, the two of you choose, and the choice must be written clearly in the deed:

- **Effective from the wedding date.** Property acquired since the wedding day is treated as separate from the start, according to the agreement.
- **Effective from the signing of the deed** (or another agreed date). Property acquired before that date remains joint property, and only what comes after is separate.

Two limits apply whichever you choose. First, Article 29(4), as interpreted by the decision, prohibits any change or revocation of the agreement that harms third parties. A bank that already holds a mortgage over your house, or a creditor who lent money while the property was joint, does not lose its rights because of a new deed. Second, the agreement only binds third parties once it is registered. Until then it binds only the two of you.

Some legal writers argue that a postnuptial agreement should not be retroactive at all, precisely to protect third parties. So discuss this choice openly with the notary, with your list of assets and debts on the table.

## Property that is already joint

A couple married for five or ten years almost always has joint property: a house, vehicles, savings from salaries. A postnuptial agreement does not simply erase that history. What a notary usually does:

1. **Lists the existing assets**, with the date each was acquired and where the money came from. Pre-marital property, gifts and inheritances stay with each spouse, but they have to be provable.
2. **Decides what happens to existing joint property.** For example, it stays joint until sold, or it is divided by written agreement.
3. **Records the debts still running**, especially mortgages and business loans, so it is clear who carries what from now on, without reducing creditors' rights.

The tidier your evidence, the stronger the agreement. Bank statements, sale deeds and inheritance certificates are worth far more than memory.

## Who usually signs one

- **Indonesian and foreign couples about to buy property.** Without separate property, the Indonesian spouse cannot hold freehold (Hak Milik) or building rights (HGB). - **Couples who married abroad and then settled in Indonesia.** The marriage has to be reported first, and only then can the agreement be registered. The sequence is in our [guide for couples who married abroad and live in Bali](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tips-hubungan/menikah-di-luar-negeri-tinggal-di-bali-lapor-nikah-dan-perjanjian-kawin).
- **One spouse is starting a business** and wants business debt kept away from the family home.
- **One spouse will receive a large inheritance or gift**, such as family land, and wants its status written down clearly.

## The steps

1. **Prepare a list of your assets and debts** together, with the evidence.
2. **Consult a notary.** The notary drafts the agreement to fit your situation, including the effective date.
3. **Sign the deed.** Both of you appear before the notary. A notary may only make deeds in the province where they are based, so choose a notary in the province where you will sign.
4. **Register the agreement** where your marriage is recorded. Muslim couples go to the KUA (Office of Religious Affairs), on the basis of a 2017 letter from the Director General of Islamic Guidance. Non-Muslim couples go to the Disdukcapil (civil registry), on the basis of Circular Letter of the Director General of Population and Civil Registration No. 472.2/5876/DUKCAPIL of 19 May 2017. The officer adds a note to your marriage certificate or marriage book. No court is involved.

An agreement can only be registered for a marriage the state has recorded. Couples who married in a religious ceremony only (nikah siri) must register the marriage first.

## Limits that still apply

- **It may not violate the law, religion or morality** (Article 29(2)).
- **It cannot decide child custody in advance.** A court decides that based on the child's interests.
- **For Muslim couples, it does not remove the husband's duty of maintenance** (Article 48 of the Compilation of Islamic Law), whatever each spouse earns.
- **It cannot be used for nominee ownership.** Article 26(2) of the Basic Agrarian Law voids any act intended to transfer freehold to a foreigner, directly or indirectly. A separation-of-property agreement protects lawful ownership by the Indonesian spouse, not disguised ownership.

On inheritance between husband and wife, a court has read full separation of property as also removing the surviving spouse's right to inherit. Read [lessons from the spousal inheritance case](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tips-hubungan/perjanjian-pranikah-dan-hak-waris-pasangan) before choosing your clauses.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can one spouse make the agreement alone?

No. A marital agreement is made by mutual consent, and both spouses sign the deed before a notary.

### Can the agreement be changed or cancelled later?

Yes, if both spouses agree and the change does not harm third parties. The change is made by a new notarial deed and registered again.

### Is a private agreement enough?

Not for registration. The civil registry only records agreements made as a notarial deed, although a private agreement on duty stamp paper can still be evidence between the two of you.

### How long does it take?

The draft usually takes a few working days, the signing fits your schedule, and registration depends on the office's queue. Realistically, two to three weeks from first consultation to registration, not counting the time to gather documents.
