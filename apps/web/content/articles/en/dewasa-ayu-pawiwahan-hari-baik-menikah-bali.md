Ask a Balinese family when their child is getting married, and the answer is almost never "we'll check the venue's schedule". It is far more often, "We are still nunas dewasa." The family is asking for guidance on a good day, and the wedding date can only be discussed once that guidance has been given.

This good day is called dewasa ayu. This article explains where dewasa ayu comes from, which elements of the calendar are weighed, which days are usually avoided for a pawiwahan, and what it means for couples who are not Balinese but want to marry on the island.

## What dewasa ayu is

Dewasa ayu is a good day for an undertaking, from building a house or starting a business to a wedding ceremony. The knowledge behind it is called wariga, the study of the character of each element of time in the Balinese calendar. From wariga comes a practical guide known as ala ayuning dewasa, literally the good and bad of days.

Wariga is written on lontar palm-leaf manuscripts, and not everyone can read it. So for an important ceremony such as a pawiwahan, families usually go to a sulinggih, a high priest, or someone versed in wariga to nunas dewasa. A study of wariga manuscripts in the village of Darmasaba notes that setting a wedding day is not done casually by laypeople and needs the guidance of a sulinggih or wariga expert. In practice, the two families then agree together on one of the good days offered.

## The calendar elements weighed

The Balinese calendar runs several cycles of time at once, and dewasa ayu is the point where those cycles meet well.

**Pawukon.** A 210-day cycle divided into 30 wuku of seven days each. Within it run ten kinds of week of different lengths at the same time, from one day to ten days long. The three mentioned most are the triwara (three days), the pancawara (five days, the same as the Javanese pasaran) and the saptawara (seven days, the same as the days of the week).

**Sasih.** Twelve months based on the moon, from Kasa to Sadha. Each sasih begins the day after the new moon, runs fifteen days to the full moon, then fifteen days back to the dark moon.

**Penanggal and pangelong.** Penanggal are the days of the waxing moon, pangelong the days of the waning moon. For ceremonies that carry a hope of growth, such as a wedding, penanggal are generally preferred.

There are also days with special names that mark particular meetings of these elements. Daily Balinese calendars list them along with their advice, including what is best not done on that day.

## One example of the rules: Lontar Panca Kanda

Each village and each wariga expert may use a different manuscript, so there is no single list that holds for all of Bali. As an illustration, here are the rules for a pawiwahan according to Lontar Panca Kanda as used in Darmasaba village, Badung, as recorded in a study from Universitas Hindu Negeri I Gusti Bagus Sugriwa.

| Element | Good for a pawiwahan | Avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Day | Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday | Sunday, Tuesday, Saturday |
| Moon phase | Penanggal 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 13 | Pangelong |
| Sasih | Best are Kapat and Kedasa; middling are Katiga, Kalima, Kapitu | Kasa, Karo, Kaenem, Kawulu, Kesanga, Jesta, Sadha |
| Other | | Rangda Tiga, Uncal Balung, wuku without a guru, Ingkel Wong, a day that falls on pasah |

What stands out about this manuscript is its flexibility. If a perfect day cannot be found, what counts as essential is the right day of the week and avoiding Rangda Tiga, Ingkel Wong and pasah. The rules on pangelong and sasih may go unmet. In other words, even the written tradition leaves room to fit reality.

## Days that often appear as "not good for marrying"

In the daily Balinese calendar, a few day names come with a firm note about weddings:

- **Rangda Tiga**, noted as not good for a pawiwahan ceremony.
- **Carik Walangati**, not good for a wedding.
- **Salah Wadi**, not good for the manusa yadnya ceremony of marriage.

Because the Balinese calendar counts many cycles at once, these days can fall on the very weekend you had in mind. That is why Balinese families check the dewasa first and book the venue afterwards.

## Where dewasa ayu fits in a pawiwahan

A Balinese Hindu wedding unfolds as a series. It begins with madewasa ayu, setting the good day, then ngidih, the formal request, followed by the rites at the groom's family home. The two most important ceremonies are the mabyakala, a purification of the bride and groom in body and spirit, and the mewidhi widana, led by a pemangku or sulinggih, which marks the marriage as religiously valid. The series closes with the mejauman, a visit to the bride's family.

A Balinese Hindu marriage is held to be valid with three witnesses, called the Tri Upasaksi: the gods as witness, people as witness, and the bhuta as witness, called upon through the mabyakala. A good day is chosen so that the whole series takes place at a time considered in harmony.

## The Balinese calendar and the name Bangbang Gde Rawi

Almost every home in Bali has a calendar far fuller than an ordinary one: it carries the pawukon, saptawara, sasih and notes of ala ayuning dewasa. One of the names most closely tied to this calendar is Ketut Bangbang Gde Rawi, a Balinese calendar expert from Celuk, Sukawati, who died in 1989. The calendar he compiled is still published today, carried on by his son and grandson. For many families, a calendar like this is the starting point before they go to nunas dewasa.

## If you're not Balinese but marrying in Bali

Many couples from Jakarta, Surabaya or abroad choose Bali as the place to marry without holding a Hindu ceremony. Dewasa ayu does not bind you religiously, but the Balinese calendar still shapes your days on the island.

**Nyepi.** On the Day of Silence the whole of Bali stops for 24 hours: no work, no fire or lights, no travel, no entertainment. Tourists may only move about within their hotel grounds, and Ngurah Rai Airport closes for 24 hours from 06:00 WITA. In 2027, Nyepi falls on Monday 8 March. That week also coincides with the Idulfitri holidays on 10 and 11 March and the collective leave days around them, so travel to and from Bali around those dates will be very busy.

**Galungan and Kuningan.** At Galungan, penjor line the roads, temples fill with prayer, and families visit one another. Galungan comes round every 210 days, and Kuningan falls ten days after it. In 2027, Galungan falls on 13 January and 11 August, with Kuningan on 23 January and 21 August. Many Balinese, including people who work in weddings, go home to their villages for these holy days. If your date is close, ask about your team's availability early.

**Ceremonies around the venue.** Temples and customary villages keep their own ceremonial calendars. If your venue is near a temple, ask the venue whether a ceremony is planned on your chosen date, especially for anything held outdoors.

## Dewasa ayu, weton and Ba Zi

Interestingly, the way Balinese choose a day has much in common with other traditions across the archipelago. The Balinese pancawara is the same as the pasaran used in [Javanese weton jodoh](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tradisi-wedding/weton-jodoh-menghitung-kecocokan-pasangan-primbon-jawa), and Sundanese families have their own [reckoning of good days in the paririmbon](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tradisi-wedding/hari-baik-menikah-tradisi-sunda-paririmbon). Chinese Indonesian families use solar terms and the sixty-fold cycle in [Ba Zi](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tradisi-wedding/mengenal-ba-zi-empat-pilar-kelahiran). All of them start from the same belief: time has a character, and important things are best begun at a time whose character is good.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who sets the dewasa ayu for a wedding?

Families usually nunas dewasa from a sulinggih, a pemangku or a wariga expert. Once they have the good days on offer, the two families agree on the date together.

### Does every village in Bali use the same rules?

Not always. The basic principles are shared because they come from wariga, but manuscripts and interpretations can differ between villages and between experts. That is why the rules from a single manuscript are best read as an example, not as a rule for everyone.

### Do non-Hindu couples need to follow dewasa ayu?

Not religiously. But it is still worth watching the Balinese calendar, especially Nyepi, Galungan and Kuningan, because those days affect travel, vendor availability and the mood around the venue.

### Can I look up dewasa ayu myself in a calendar?

Daily Balinese calendars list good days and days best avoided. For a wedding, the calendar is a fine starting point, but Balinese Hindu families still ask a sulinggih or wariga expert before deciding.
