Being invited to a Balinese wedding, or marrying into a Balinese family, tends to leave people from outside the island curious and a little anxious. What is the tooth-filing ceremony people mention? Why are there offerings in every corner? What should you wear, and what should you avoid?

This article answers those questions. The ceremony itself, from the proposal to the farewell to the bride's ancestors, is covered step by step in our [guide to the stages of a Balinese wedding](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tradisi-wedding/pernikahan-adat-bali-tahapan-dan-makna). Here the focus is on the traditions around it: what you will see, what may be asked of you, and how to show respect.

## Metatah: tooth filing before marriage

Metatah, also called mepandes or mesangih, is the ceremony of filing the tips of the upper teeth level. It is a manusa yadnya, a life-cycle rite marking the passage from childhood to adulthood, and for Balinese Hindus it is an obligatory one.

Six upper teeth are filed: the four incisors and the two canines. They stand for the **sad ripu**, the six enemies within a person:

- **Kama**, lust.
- **Lobha**, greed.
- **Krodha**, anger.
- **Mada**, intoxication, including intoxication with pleasure.
- **Moha**, confusion or delusion.
- **Matsarya**, jealousy.

Even teeth represent an even temperament, while sharp canines are seen as a sign of animal nature. The ceremony is led by a pemangku or sulinggih (priest) with mantras and holy water, and in some places ends with tasting six flavours, from sweet to bitter, as a reminder that adult life holds all of them.

**When does it happen, and what does it have to do with marriage?** Ideally metatah takes place as a child comes of age. One source gives an age range of 6 to 18. But the ceremony is costly, and families often wait until several children are ready so they can hold it together. As a result, quite a few Balinese have not yet had metatah when they come to marry, and the ceremony is held shortly before the wedding or combined with another ceremony to save on costs.

So if your Balinese partner mentions "metatah first, then the wedding", that is perfectly normal. For partners from outside Bali joining a Balinese Hindu family, a report in NOW! Bali says that Western women marrying a Balinese man need to undergo metatah before they can enter the family. Practice differs between families, so talk it through directly with your partner's family.

## Banten: offerings everywhere

The first thing visitors usually notice is banten, offerings made of palm leaf, flowers, fruit, cakes and incense. Canang, the small daily offerings, sit at doorways, on shrines, on the road and even on car dashboards. At a wedding the offerings are far larger and more numerous, and each kind has its own role in the ceremony, such as the byakaon offering at the purification rite, or the ketipat bantal brought when the bride takes leave of her ancestors.

For guests there are two practical points:

- **Do not step over or on offerings** placed on the ground, including canang at doorways and on the road. If it happens by accident, there is no need to panic; simply take more care.
- **Do not touch or move offerings** in the ceremony area, and do not use them as a photo backdrop without asking.

## Payas agung, madya and alit

Among the forms of Balinese traditional dress, there are three names you will hear often around a wedding.

| Dress | What it looks like | Worn for |
|---|---|---|
| **Payas agung** | The most opulent: a large headdress and gilded prada cloth | Weddings, by the bride and groom only. Also worn at other major ceremonies such as metatah. |
| **Payas madya** | One level below payas agung and more relaxed: a shirt or collared top with kamen (sarong) and a selendang or senteng (sash) | Less formal customary occasions |
| **Payas alit** | Kebaya, kamen and selendang for women; a white or safari shirt, kamen and udeng (headcloth) for men | Village meetings and temple activities |

**Payas agung** is the Balinese bridal dress most people picture. For the bride, a study of Bali Agung bridal make-up from Universitas Negeri Surabaya records hair dressed in a tall cone, the gelung agung headdress, inspired by a mountain and standing for majesty, a petitis ornament on the forehead, the garuda mungkur at the back of the head, and gold sandat flowers. The costume uses gilded prada cloth, a prada sash and jewellery such as gelang kana armlets and subeng earrings. According to the same study, it signals that the couple are adults in body and spirit, ready to step out from their parents and take on new rights and duties.

If you plan to wear payas agung for photos or a reception and want a particular regional style, say payas agung from Badung or Buleleng, ask the make-up artist whether that is a style they know.

**Payas alit** is what people wear to the temple, and it is the most appropriate choice if guests are asked to come in traditional dress.

One simple rule: **do not dress like the couple.** The red, gold and white with a large headdress belong to the bride and groom.

## Etiquette for guests

Our full guide on [what to wear to a Bali wedding](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/beauty-fashion/baju-kondangan-ke-bali-musim-hujan), including fabrics that cope with the wet season, covers clothing in detail. In short:

- **Ask about the dress code.** Some families expect guests in kamen and selendang, others do not mind. If the ceremony is at a temple or family compound, a kamen and selendang are almost always right.
- **Avoid revealing clothes.** Shoulders and knees are best covered in the ceremony area.
- **Women who are menstruating do not enter the temple area.** This is a general rule at Balinese temples. Let the family know politely if you need to wait outside.
- **Follow the pemangku or the ceremony organisers.** Do not photograph parts of the ceremony that may not be photographed, and do not block people who are praying.
- **Plan time off.** A Balinese wedding date is chosen by [dewasa ayu, the good-day calendar](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tradisi-wedding/dewasa-ayu-pawiwahan-hari-baik-menikah-bali), not by the weekend, so it may fall on a weekday.
- **On gifts, ask.** As at Indonesian weddings generally, an envelope is often the most practical choice. If you are close family, the family may expect something else, so ask them directly.

## For non-Balinese couples marrying in Bali

Many couples from Jakarta, Singapore, Australia and beyond fall in love with Bali and want to bring something of it into their wedding. That can be done respectfully.

**Separate culture from religious rite.** The pawiwahan is a Hindu ceremony. Couples who are not Hindu marry according to their own religion, then bring in elements of Balinese culture such as payas agung for a photo session, gamelan, a welcome dance or palm-leaf decoration. Avoid restaging rites such as the mabyakala purification as a performance.

**Respect the Balinese calendar.** Nyepi, Galungan and Kuningan affect travel, vendors and the atmosphere around your venue. The 2027 dates are in our [dewasa ayu article](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tradisi-wedding/dewasa-ayu-pawiwahan-hari-baik-menikah-bali).

**Ask about temples near the venue.** If your venue is near a temple, ask whether a ceremony is planned on your date, especially for an outdoor ceremony.

**If one of you is Balinese,** the form of marriage decides who joins whose family, and that affects property. Couples who want to keep their property clearly separate can sign a [marital agreement with a notary in Bali](https://7magicwedding.com/perjanjian-pranikah-bali).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is tooth filing part of the wedding ceremony?

No. Metatah is a separate coming-of-age rite. But because it should ideally happen before marriage, it is often held shortly before the wedding for anyone who has not had it yet.

### Does metatah hurt?

Only the tips of the teeth are filed, and the act is symbolic. Despite its English name, it does not remove or cut the teeth down.

### Can non-Hindu guests wear a kebaya and kamen?

Yes, and it is usually appreciated. A kamen and selendang are easy to buy in Bali. The only thing to avoid is dressing like the couple.

### Can I take photos of the ceremony?

That depends on the family and on the part of the ceremony. Ask first, turn off the flash, and do not stand in front of people who are praying.
