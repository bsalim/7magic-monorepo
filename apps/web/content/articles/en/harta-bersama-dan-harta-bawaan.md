Most couples in Indonesia marry without a prenuptial agreement. That does not mean their property is unregulated. The law already provides default rules, and those rules apply until the two of you decide otherwise.

The trouble is that many people only read these rules when something goes wrong: the house is up for sale, a debt is being collected, or a spouse has died. This article explains them earlier, so you can judge whether the defaults suit you or need changing with an agreement.

## Three kinds of property in a marriage

Article 35 of Law No. 1 of 1974 on Marriage divides a couple's property into three:

- **Joint property**, meaning property acquired during the marriage. That includes both spouses' salaries, savings from those salaries, and anything bought with that money, in whoever's name.
- **Pre-marital property**, meaning what each spouse owned before the wedding.
- **Gifts and inheritances** received by each spouse, even during the marriage.

Pre-marital property, gifts and inheritances stay under each spouse's control, "as long as the parties do not determine otherwise". That last phrase is the room a marital agreement uses.

For Muslim couples, the Compilation of Islamic Law (KHI) uses the same division and confirms that, in principle, a husband's and wife's property do not merge because of the marriage. The dowry (mahar), for example, is the wife's personal property from the moment it is handed over. We cover it in [mahar in Islam](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/pernikahan-islami/mahar-dalam-islam).

## Who may do what

For joint property, Article 36(1) of the Marriage Law requires the consent of both spouses. KHI Article 92 puts it more firmly: neither spouse may sell or transfer joint property without the other's consent. In practice, this is why notaries and banks ask for your spouse's signature when you sell or mortgage the house.

For pre-marital property, each spouse has full rights (Article 36(2)). You can sell an apartment you bought before the wedding without your spouse's consent, provided its pre-marital status can be proven.

## Where the line often blurs

The rules above sound tidy. In real life the line often blurs for three reasons:

1. **Mixed money.** A pre-marital apartment is sold, and the proceeds are topped up with joint savings to buy a house. The house is now partly pre-marital and partly joint, and the proportions have to be proven.
2. **Income from pre-marital property.** The Marriage Law does not say clearly whether rent from a pre-marital house, or dividends from inherited shares, count as joint property. Expert opinion differs.
3. **Lost evidence.** Years later it is hard to show that a plot of land was bought with inherited money rather than salary.

For all three the answer is the same: keep evidence of where the money came from, or write the status down in a marital agreement.

## Debts: who carries them

The law does not make every debt a joint debt. For Muslim couples, KHI Article 93 sets out tiers:

- A spouse's personal debt is paid from that spouse's own property.
- Debt taken on for the family is paid from joint property.
- If joint property is not enough, it is paid from the husband's property, then from the wife's.

Hukumonline also explains that a personal debt taken on without the spouse's consent cannot be collected from that spouse's personal property. Again the problem is proof: without an agreement, a creditor can argue that the debt was for family needs, or that an item is joint property. For couples where one spouse runs a business on borrowed capital, this is the most common reason to sign an agreement. ## If the marriage ends

**By divorce.** Article 37 of the Marriage Law says joint property is settled "according to the respective law", meaning the religious law, customary law or other law that applies to the couple. For Muslim couples, KHI Article 97 gives each spouse half of the joint property, unless the marriage agreement says otherwise. Judges do not always split it evenly, especially when the two spouses' contributions were very unequal.

**By death.** For Muslim couples, KHI Article 96 gives half of the joint property to the surviving spouse. The other half, plus the deceased's pre-marital property, becomes the estate, divided among the heirs, including the surviving spouse. A certificate in the surviving spouse's name does not change this calculation. We walk through an example in [a house in the wife's or husband's name](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tips-hubungan/rumah-atas-nama-istri-atau-suami-milik-siapa).

For non-Muslim couples, the division follows the law that applies to them. If you are not sure which law applies to you, ask the notary directly.

## When a marital agreement changes all this

A marital agreement can change almost every default above: separate all property, separate only part of it (a family business, for example), or settle the status of income from pre-marital property. It can be made before the wedding or, since 2016, during the marriage. How the postnuptial version works is in our [guide to postnuptial agreements](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tips-hubungan/perjanjian-kawin-setelah-menikah).

What an agreement cannot change: the husband's duty of maintenance in an Islamic marriage, child custody, and the existing rights of third parties.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the wife's salary joint property?

Yes, if there is no agreement. Both spouses' earnings during the marriage are joint property, even when kept in accounts in their own names.

### Are wedding gifts from parents joint property?

A gift given to one spouse stays that spouse's property. A gift given to both of you is usually treated as joint. If parents want their gift, such as land, to belong only to their child, they should say so clearly in the deed of gift.

### Do debts from before the marriage become my spouse's burden?

No. A debt taken on before the wedding is a personal debt and is paid from the borrowing spouse's property.

### Are prenuptial agreements only for the wealthy?

No. The couples who gain most are often those with a small business running on loans, those who will receive family land, or those marrying a foreign national.

## Next steps

Sit down together and write three lists: each spouse's pre-marital property, what you expect to receive from family, and the debts you have or plan.
