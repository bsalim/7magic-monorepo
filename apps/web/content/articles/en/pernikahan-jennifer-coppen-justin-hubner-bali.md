On Sunday afternoon, 14 June 2026, at Puri Bhagawan in Jimbaran, Jennifer Coppen and Justin Hubner said their vows as the sun went down. Two days earlier they had already married legally in a Javanese akad in Uluwatu. This second day had a different feel: a different gown, a different make-up artist, and a reception in red and gold after the ceremony.

The wedding of Jennifer and Justin, a footballer, ran over four days in Bali across two venues. The pattern is useful if you're planning a wedding away from home: a solemn akad on day one, then a second day that's freer, for celebrating with guests.

## How the days ran

- **Wednesday, 24 December 2025**: the engagement is announced. The proposal took place at Kasteel Engelenburg in Brummen, the Netherlands, with Jennifer's daughter Kamari present.
- **Thursday, 11 June 2026**: siraman and pengajian (a Quran recital with prayers) at Tirtha Bali.
- **Friday, 12 June 2026**: the akad nikah at Tirtha Bali, Uluwatu, in Javanese adat.
- **Saturday, 13 June 2026**: SCTV broadcasts the akad.
- **Sunday, 14 June 2026**: sunset vows, then the reception, at Puri Bhagawan, Jimbaran.
- **Monday, 15 June 2026**: SCTV broadcasts the reception.

There was also a welcome dinner for guests, though its exact date wasn't announced. From the engagement announcement to the akad took under six months, and every core event fell within a single Thursday-to-Sunday stretch.

## A proposal that included a child

Justin proposed to Jennifer at Kasteel Engelenburg in Brummen, the Netherlands, and Kamari was there. That small detail matters to couples who bring a child into a marriage. When a child witnesses the proposal, they see their new family take shape and know their place in it from day one.

If you're in a similar position, think about your child's role at each stage, from the proposal to the wedding day. It can be simple, such as standing beside you during the vows or carrying the rings, as long as it suits their age and comfort.

## An all-white Javanese akad

The akad at Tirtha Bali, Uluwatu, followed Javanese adat. Jennifer wore a white kebaya and Justin a beskap, the Javanese formal jacket, both by Maison Baaz Couture. The akad make-up was by Kun Soo. Erick Thohir, chairman of PSSI, the Indonesian football association, acted as a witness.

Choosing Javanese adat for an akad in Bali shows that the location doesn't have to dictate the adat. What you need to prepare is the ceremonial kit itself and someone who knows the order of the rites, since both usually have to come from your home city. Ask your WO or bridal stylist early whether they can travel to the venue, and who covers the trip.

## An 18-gram mahar and €2,026

Justin's mahar to Jennifer was 18 grams of gold, made up of 12 grams and 6 grams, plus €2,026 in cash. The 12 and the 6 are the day and month of the akad, and 2,026 is the year. Added together, the two pieces give one round figure, 18 grams.

Splitting the grams like this helps if you want a mahar that encodes your date but would still like to name a single round number. Pick a currency that means something to you both, or simply use rupiah with the same figure.

## Day two: sunset vows, then the reception

On Sunday the celebration moved to Puri Bhagawan in Jimbaran. For the sunset vows, Jennifer wore a gown by Christie Basil with make-up by Leo Afandi. For the reception that followed she changed into a Hian Tjen gown, and the setting was red and gold.

There are some practical lessons here. A sunset ceremony needs a schedule counted back carefully, because the light is only good for a short while. Changing gowns between ceremony and reception also needs a dressing room and enough of a gap. And booking a different make-up artist for each look makes sense if you want a different character for each, as long as their schedules don't collide.

Notice the shift in colour too. The akad was all white, the reception red and gold. Giving each day its own palette makes the photos from the two days feel distinct, and guests who attend both get something new.

## Welcome dinner and documentation

A wedding away from home often includes a welcome event. Jennifer and Justin held a welcome dinner too. An event like this gives guests who've travelled a chance to get to know each other before the big day. For your own wedding it needn't be lavish. A relaxed dinner at a restaurant near where guests are staying is enough.

Photos and video were by FCG Weddings. For a four-day event across two venues, one documentation team covering every day gives the final result a consistent style. Make sure the contract states in writing the number of days, the number of locations and the team's travel costs.

The couple's akad and reception were also broadcast on SCTV, each a day after the event. A TV broadcast is plainly not an option for most people, but the need behind it is the same: relatives who can't fly to Bali still want to watch. A private livestream behind a link, or a short video sent to the family group chat the next day, meets that need. Raise it with your documentation team early if you want it, because it takes extra people and equipment.

## What you can borrow

- For a destination wedding, gather the core events into a few consecutive days so guests only need to take leave once.
- Bring your family's adat to the destination. Arrange the kit and a stylist who knows the order of the rites.
- Split the grams of your mahar into two figures if you want to encode your date in one round total.
- If you choose a sunset ceremony, ask your WO to build the schedule back from the time of sunset.
- Give each day of the celebration its own colour palette.
- If one of you already has a child, give them a role they're comfortable with at each stage.
