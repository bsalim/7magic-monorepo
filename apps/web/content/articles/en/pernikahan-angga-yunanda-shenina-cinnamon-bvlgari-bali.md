On Monday, 10 February 2025, most of Angga Yunanda and Shenina Cinnamon's fans only found out the two had married once it was all over. There was no coverage of wedding plans beforehand, no leaked date. The first report came out that same day, complete with photos of the akad, the Islamic marriage ceremony, at Bvlgari Resort Bali.

Those photos showed the couple in Minang traditional dress that was almost entirely white, with silver accents. Angga later shared the moment on Instagram with a short caption: "Forever and always".

We're not here to discuss their private lives. What's interesting for couples planning a wedding are the decisions behind it: keeping the guest list small, choosing a tradition and then interpreting it in a calm colour palette, and tucking the wedding date into the mahar, the marriage gift from groom to bride. All of it works on a far more ordinary budget.

## How the day unfolded

- **Monday, 10 February 2025.** An Islamic akad at Bvlgari Resort Bali, in all-white and silver Minang attire.
- **The same day.** A private reception for family and close friends. Shenina changed into a white gown by Hian Tjen.
- **The same day.** News of the wedding reached the media for the first time, with no coverage of any preparations beforehand.

## A surprise wedding with a short guest list

Only family and close friends attended the akad and the reception. To many couples that sounds impossible. Guest lists in Indonesia tend to grow on their own, because every parent has colleagues, neighbours and distant relatives who feel they ought to be invited.

The lesson here lies in the order of things. The decision about scale came first, and the venue and other details followed. If you want a small wedding, agree on the number with both families before anyone starts spreading the news. Once lots of people know the date, holding the guest list down gets much harder.

Keeping the news quiet until the day has practical advantages too. No extra guests turn up because they heard from someone else, and the vendors can work without interruption. For relatives you couldn't invite, send the news and a photo by private message before you post it on social media. That feels far warmer than having them find out from their feed.

## Minang attire in white and silver

Both of their akad outfits were designed by Didiet Maulana in the Minang tradition. What set them apart was the colour. Minang bridal attire is more often seen in strong colours such as red and gold, while here nearly everything was white with silver accents.

Angga wore **songket balapak**, a woven cloth typical of the Minang, as part of his outfit. That way the traditional element still read clearly even though the palette was much quieter.

If you want to honour your family's tradition but find its traditional colours too busy for the look you have in mind, this is a good example of a middle path. Keep the distinctive shapes and cloth, then talk about the palette with your designer. It's also wise to ask your parents or a respected elder whether a colour change like this is acceptable in your family. Better to ask before the cloth is cut than to explain after the photos are out.

## A mahar that holds the date

The mahar was **10 grams and 2 grams** of gold bullion, plus **US$2,025** in cash. Read in order, the numbers spell out the date of the akad: 10/2/2025.

The idea is easy to copy and doesn't have to be expensive. The weights can be scaled to what you can afford, as long as the numbers still read as a date. A wedding on the 3rd of August, for example, could use 3 grams and 8 grams of gold. Foreign currency isn't required either. Rupiah in the amount of the wedding year carries plenty of meaning.

Bear in mind that the mahar belongs to the bride. Discuss its form and amount between the two of you first, before it goes to the two families.

## A second gown for the reception

For the reception, Shenina changed into a white gown designed by Hian Tjen. Two outfits for two parts of the day is common now, and here the split was clear. Traditional attire for the akad, a gown for the reception.

This pattern makes planning easier. Traditional attire for the akad can be rented or borrowed from family, while the clothing budget goes on the one reception gown you really want. Just make sure there's enough time and a changing space between the two events.

## A styling team split by person

Behind their looks were several names with different jobs. Shenina's makeup was done by Marlene Hariman, while her hair was handled by Jooe Yunedi. Angga had his own makeup artist, Ryan Ogilvy.

Couples rarely think about a split like this, yet it matters. In a standard makeup package, one team often has to handle the couple, the parents and sometimes the bridal party all at once. If your schedule is tight, ask your makeup vendor how many people they'll bring and who does what. The groom belongs in that conversation too, because he'll be photographed just as often.

## What you can borrow

- **Decide on scale before choosing a venue.** A guest number agreed early shapes every other decision.
- **Keep the news quiet until the day if you want a small event.** Word-of-mouth invitations are the hardest source of extra guests to control.
- **Interpret tradition through colour.** You can keep the shape of the attire and the distinctive cloth while choosing a palette that suits your taste.
- **Tuck the date into the mahar.** The grams of gold or the cash amount can be scaled to your budget, and the meaning stays the same.
- **Split outfits by event.** Traditional attire for the akad, a gown for the reception, with the budget focused on what you care about most.
- **Ask how the styling team divides the work.** Make sure the groom and the bride's hair aren't treated as side jobs.
