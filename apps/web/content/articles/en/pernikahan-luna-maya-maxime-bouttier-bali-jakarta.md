At one of the most personal moments of her wedding celebrations, Luna Maya wore a batik cloth bearing a painting by her late father, Uut Bambang Sugeng. The cloth was made by Asri Welas for the melukat, a Balinese purification ritual. Her father couldn't be there, but his work was.

Small details like that are what make Luna Maya and Maxime Bouttier's wedding worth studying. The celebrations ran long: a siraman in Ubud, a Yogyakarta-style akad in Payangan, a reception in Bali, then a second reception in Jakarta almost three months later. Each stage had its own outfits, team and mood.

You may not be planning events on two islands. Still, the way they split the events, brought in a parent who has passed away and put together their wedding favours can all be applied at a much smaller scale.

## How the celebrations unfolded

- **Tuesday, 1 April 2025.** News of the proposal is shared. Maxime proposed to Luna in Tokyo.
- **Tuesday, 6 May 2025.** Siraman, the ritual bathing, and sungkeman, asking the parents' blessing, at The Ubud Village Resort.
- **Wednesday, 7 May 2025.** The akad in the afternoon at COMO Shambhala Estate, Payangan, Gianyar, Bali.
- **Thursday, 8 May 2025.** An internationally themed reception, with both of them dressed in white.
- **Wednesday, 30 July 2025.** A second reception at Four Seasons Hotel Jakarta.

## The siraman, and a cloth to remember her father

For the siraman, Luna wore a pink jumputan outfit designed by Fadlan. Jumputan is a tie-dyed cloth, and its soft colour gave the ritual a light feel.

The cloth for the melukat had a different story. Asri Welas designed a batik featuring a painting by Luna's father. That way, her father was present without an empty chair or a framed photo in the corner of the room.

If one of your parents has passed away, this is an approach worth considering. Handwriting, a motif from a favourite cloth or a drawing they once made can be printed or embroidered onto fabric, a handkerchief or the lining of a jacket. Talk to the craftsperson or designer early, because work like this takes time.

## An akad in the Yogyakarta tradition

The akad followed the Javanese tradition in the Yogyakarta style, with **paes ageng** bridal makeup. The akad kebaya was designed by Eddy Betty and paired with **Sidomulyo** batik, while the makeup was done by Andy Chun. Raffi Ahmad and Irwan Mussry served as witnesses.

What stands out here is the consistency. The location was Bali, but the chosen tradition stayed Javanese, complete with the paes and the batik. The venue doesn't have to decide the tradition. If you're marrying far from your family's hometown, make sure the makeup artist and designer you choose really know that tradition, and ask whether they can travel to the location.

Choosing the witnesses deserves early thought as well. Witnesses are a legal part of the akad, so pick people who can definitely be there on the set date and time, especially for a wedding out of town.

## A 7.5-gram mahar

The mahar was **7.5 grams** of gold bullion and **US$2,025** in cash. The 2,025 clearly points to the year, and several outlets read the 7.5 grams as the date of the akad, 7 May.

A mahar that holds the date like this is easy to scale to your budget. The figure after the decimal point in the gold weight can stand for the month, and the cash can be in rupiah. What matters is that the bride and groom agree from the start.

## The Bali reception and a gown painted with her parents

The reception in Bali had an international theme, and the couple both wore white. In Bali, Luna also wore a white gown designed by Hian Tjen.

The most memorable moment came at the after-party. Luna wore a black gown by Yefta Gunawan painted with portraits of her father and her mother. The same idea as the melukat cloth appeared again, this time in a bolder form.

An after-party is useful as a separate space. The formal event stays orderly and true to tradition, while the more relaxed, personal part gets its own time. On a tight budget, the after-party can simply be a small dinner with close friends once the reception is over.

## A second reception in Jakarta

Almost three months after the akad, they held a reception at Four Seasons Hotel Jakarta. Invitations went out to 500 people, and nearly 800 came. Luna wore a gown designed by Sebastian Gunawan, then changed into a second look by Yogie Pratama. Her makeup in Jakarta was done by Archangela Chelsea.

There are two lessons here. The first is that separating an out-of-town akad from a reception in your home city gives room to both. The akad can stay small, while colleagues and relatives who didn't come to Bali still get a chance to offer their congratulations.

The second is about guest numbers. The gap between invitations and attendance can be far wider than expected, especially for a reception in the city where the family lives. Ask your venue and caterer how many extra portions they recommend, and how they handle additional guests on the day.

## Favours that get used

The favours at the Jakarta reception included an engraved coffee machine, ZEN Tableware and skincare products. All of them are things guests will use at home, not ornaments.

This principle works whatever your budget. A single useful item is often more appreciated by guests than several trinkets that end up in a drawer.

## What you can borrow

- **Separate the akad and the reception if the guests differ.** A small akad in the place you dream of, a big reception in your family's city.
- **Bring in a parent who has passed away through their work.** Handwriting, a painting or a favourite motif can become cloth or a detail on an outfit.
- **Choose vendors who know your tradition, not ones near the venue.** Ask early whether they can travel to the location.
- **Plan a buffer for unexpected guests.** Agree on the extra numbers with your caterer and venue well before the day.
- **Choose favours that get used.** One useful item is remembered longer than a pile of ornaments.
