On a clifftop in Uluwatu just before sunset, an elopement usually involves four people: the couple, a celebrant who leads the vows, and a photographer. No wedding stage, no receiving line, no guest list cut down again and again. It is over in half an hour, followed by dinner for two.

Plenty of Indonesian couples are drawn to that picture, then immediately ask two things: is this the same as kawin lari, and is the marriage legal? This article answers both, and explains what you need to arrange if you want to do it in Bali.

## What elopement means, then and now

The English word *elope* did originally mean running away to marry, usually without the parents' blessing. The example most often cited comes from 18th-century England. Lord Hardwicke's Marriage Act, in force from 1754, required a church ceremony, banns or a special licence, and parental consent for anyone under 21. It applied only to England and Wales. Scotland was left out, so young couples crossed the border, and Gretna Green, the first village past the line, became a famous place for quick weddings that it remains today.

The meaning has shifted a long way. Today an elopement is a wedding kept deliberately small: just the couple, or the couple with a handful of the people closest to them, often somewhere far from home. Parents usually know, and often give their blessing. What gets left behind is the reception, not the family.

That is why "kawin lari" is a poor translation. In Indonesia the phrase suggests marrying in secret without anyone's blessing. In Lombok, kawin lari even has a specific customary meaning: *merariq* among the Sasak people is a tradition of taking the bride away, with clear rules and stages, including a formal notice to her family. It is a tradition in its own right and has nothing to do with the elopement trend. When you explain your plans to family, "elopement" or "nikah berdua" (marrying just the two of us) is a safer choice of words than "kawin lari".

## Elopement, micro wedding and intimate wedding

There is no official definition, and vendors use all three terms loosely. A practical way to tell them apart:

- **Elopement:** the couple alone, or with a few others. A handy test is whether everyone present fits around one dinner table. The day centres on the ceremony and the photos, not a banquet.
- **Micro wedding:** roughly a dozen to a few dozen guests, usually close family and best friends. There is a shared meal and a bit of a running order.
- **Intimate wedding:** in Indonesia this often means a small reception with dozens of guests, up to about a hundred. The structure resembles a normal reception, just shorter. We break down the costs in our [guide to a 100-guest intimate wedding in Jakarta](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/venue-lokasi/intimate-wedding-100-tamu-jakarta-rincian-biaya).

The difference you feel most is not the number of guests but who you plan around. A reception is designed for the guests. An elopement is designed for the two of you, and almost every decision, from the time of the ceremony to what you wear, can be made without thinking about anyone else.

## Why couples choose it, and what they give up

The reasons heard most often are money and energy. A reception for hundreds of guests in a big city absorbs a budget that could go towards a house deposit, and the preparation can take a year. There are also couples who are uncomfortable being the centre of attention for hundreds of people, couples marrying for the second time, and couples whose families are spread over several countries, so that a big reception is actually hard to attend.

What you give up is real too. Rituals that involve the family, such as sungkeman, siraman or the tea pai, cannot be done by two people. Parents who have long pictured hosting a reception for their child may feel they have lost that moment. Many couples end up choosing a middle path: the akad or church blessing with family in their home city, then an elopement in Bali as their own private ceremony.

## Is an elopement legal?

Under Article 2 of Law No. 1 of 1974 on Marriage, a marriage is valid when it is performed according to the law of each party's religion and then registered by the state. In other words, a clifftop ceremony with a celebrant reading vows does not automatically make a valid marriage. Most Bali elopement packages say so openly: the celebrant is *non-legal*. The ceremony is symbolic, and the legal part has to be arranged separately.

How you arrange it depends on your religion.

### For Muslim couples

The Compilation of Islamic Law sets out five pillars of marriage: the groom, the bride, the bride's guardian (wali), two witnesses, and the ijab kabul exchange. So a valid akad cannot be performed by the two of you alone. The smallest possible line-up is the couple, the wali (usually the bride's father), two witnesses, and the registrar (penghulu).

There are two common patterns:

1. **Akad in your home city, elopement in Bali.** An akad at the KUA during working hours is free of charge, and your parents can attend without having to fly. Then the two of you travel to Bali for the symbolic ceremony and photos.
2. **Akad in Bali.** This is possible, but the wali and two witnesses must come too, and you need a recommendation letter (often called a surat numpang nikah) from the KUA for the address on your ID card in order to register at the KUA where the akad will take place. An akad outside the KUA office, for example at a villa or hotel, costs Rp600,000 under Government Regulation No. 59 of 2018. Start on the paperwork a few months ahead, as each KUA's requirements can differ slightly.

### For non-Muslim couples

The marriage is performed according to your religion, for example a church blessing or a ceremony at a temple, and then registered with the Population and Civil Registry Office (Disdukcapil). Many couples arrange the blessing and registration in their home city, where their church or minister is, and make Bali the setting for a second, more private ceremony. If you want a legally valid blessing in Bali, ask two things early on: whether a minister from your church is willing to travel or a local church will officiate, and how the registration is passed on to Disdukcapil.

### For interfaith couples

Eloping to Bali changes nothing here. Indonesian law requires a marriage to be performed according to religion, and since July 2023, Supreme Court Circular No. 2 of 2023 has instructed courts not to grant applications to register interfaith marriages. Disdukcapil has also confirmed it will not register one without a court order. A symbolic ceremony is still possible, but do not treat it as a legal way around the rule.

## If one or both of you are foreign nationals

The basic rule is the same: both partners must share a religion, and it must be one recognised in Indonesia. The foreign partner usually needs a certificate of no impediment to marriage from their embassy or consulate, a passport, a birth certificate, and proof of divorce or a previous spouse's death if they were married before. All foreign documents are translated into Indonesian by a sworn translator. For non-Muslim couples, civil registration involves a waiting period of about 10 days after the notice of intent to marry is published.

Because the process is long, many foreign couples choose to complete the legal marriage at home and then hold a symbolic ceremony in Bali. If one of you is Indonesian, there is one more thing to settle before the wedding day: a separation of property agreement. Without it, the Indonesian partner can lose the right to own land in their own name. We explain it in our [guide to marrying a foreign national and the prenup](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tips-hubungan/perjanjian-pranikah-perkawinan-campuran-wna).

## What an elopement in Bali looks like

The choice of setting is what makes Bali popular. Limestone cliffs at Uluwatu and Melasti Beach, the beaches of Nusa Dua and Jimbaran, rice fields and valleys around Ubud, waterfalls such as Tibumana, private villas, even a boat sailing at sunset.

Packages look much the same from one vendor to the next. As an example, Go Real Escapes, one of the companies selling elopement packages in Bali, lists a floral arch, a bouquet and boutonniere, a non-legal English-speaking celebrant, and a photographer for two hours. Its prices run from Rp18.4 million for a villa ceremony to Rp33 million for a catamaran, with no validity date given. The villa package does not include renting the villa. Compare details like these before choosing, and for an overall picture of wedding costs in Bali, see our [comparison of villas, hotels and beach clubs](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/venue-lokasi/menikah-di-bali-budget-masuk-akal).

A few things that are often missed:

- **Location fees.** Some cliffs and beaches belong to individuals, villages or hotels. The Uluwatu package we read includes a landowner fee. Ask whether this fee is included.
- **Rules at sacred sites.** Bali Governor's Circular No. 7 of 2025 asks visitors to dress modestly at sacred sites and not to enter the main areas of a temple except to pray. If you want photos at a temple, ask its custodians or the local customary village (desa adat) for permission first. A site-by-site list of rules is in our [article on unusual pre-wedding locations in Bali](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/photography/lokasi-prewedding-unik-bali).
- **The foreign tourist levy.** Since February 2024, foreign visitors pay Rp150,000 per person when they visit Bali. Indonesian citizens are exempt, but if your partner or guests are foreign nationals, remind them.

## When to go

The dry season, roughly May to October, gives the best weather and light for photos, which makes it the busiest time. Vendors recommend booking 6 to 12 months ahead if you are aiming for those months. The wet season, November to March, still works as long as the location has a covered backup plan.

One date to avoid is Nyepi. According to the joint ministerial decree on 2027 public holidays, the Day of Silence falls on Monday 8 March 2027. During Nyepi the whole of Bali stops for 24 hours: the airport closes, the roads are empty, and visitors may not leave their accommodation. 2027 has an extra wrinkle, because Eid al-Fitr and its collective leave days fall close by. From Saturday 6 March to Monday 15 March 2027 many people are off work, so flights and hotels in Bali are likely to be full and expensive.

## Talking it through with your parents

To many parents, "we want to get married just the two of us in Bali" sounds like a rejection. They may have long imagined welcoming guests, or feel obliged to invite relatives who once invited them. It is easier to discuss if you come with a plan that includes them.

A few approaches that often work: involve your parents in the akad or blessing in your home city, stream the Bali ceremony over a video call, or hold a small thanksgiving gathering (syukuran) after you return for relatives who want to congratulate you. Let each of you speak to your own family, in the language you use at home. Give a concrete reason, for example that you want to put the reception budget towards a home, rather than a vague one like "to keep it simple".

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an elopement the same as nikah siri?

No. Nikah siri is a religious marriage that is not registered with the state. An elopement is only about the size of the event. An elopement can be fully legal if the akad or blessing is performed and registered, whether in Bali or in your home city.

### Can we marry legally with just the two of us in Bali?

For Muslim couples, no, because the akad requires a wali and two witnesses. For non-Muslim couples, the blessing and civil registration also require a minister and witnesses. What the two of you can do alone is the symbolic ceremony.

### How long does it take to prepare?

A symbolic ceremony can be arranged in a few weeks if the date and location are still available. The slow part is the paperwork for the legal marriage, especially if it involves transferring to another KUA or a foreign partner. Allow at least three months.

### Can our parents come?

Of course. Many elopements in Bali are attended by parents and siblings. Once the guest count grows, make sure the location has room and that the permit covers that number of people.
