A Hukumonline reader, an Indonesian living in Japan with a Japanese spouse, wanted to buy a house in Indonesia with a mortgage. The bank asked for a document they did not have: a certificate of separation of property. They had never made a marriage agreement, so the reader asked: are mixed couples completely unable to own a home in Indonesia, even in the Indonesian partner's name?

Stories like this show that a prenuptial agreement is about more than divorce. It shapes how a bank assesses you, who can be pursued for a debt, and what you can buy in your own name. If you are new to the basics, start with our [complete guide to prenuptial agreements](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tips-hubungan/perjanjian-pranikah-panduan-lengkap). This article covers what usually only comes up after the deed is signed.

## The basic rules in one paragraph

Under Article 35 of the Marriage Law, property acquired during the marriage becomes joint property, while property brought into the marriage, gifts and inheritances stay under each spouse's control unless agreed otherwise. Article 36 adds that a spouse needs the other's consent to act on joint property. Marriage agreements are governed by Article 29. Since Constitutional Court Decision 69/PUU-XIII/2015, read on 27 October 2016, the agreement may be made before the wedding, at the wedding, or at any time during the marriage.

## Separate property and mortgages: not always an advantage

Many couples assume separating property is automatically safer. With a mortgage, it can work the other way.

A *joint income* mortgage, which combines both salaries so you can borrow more, generally requires that the couple has no separation-of-property agreement. If your property is separate, the bank usually counts only the main applicant's income. For a young couple on similar salaries, that difference can decide whether the house you want is within reach.

On the other hand, without separation, a house bought during the marriage is joint property. The bank will ask for your spouse's signature on the mortgage security, and selling or remortgaging the house later also needs your spouse's consent.

Mixed couples are in a different position again. An Indonesian married to a foreigner without separation of property cannot hold freehold (Hak Milik) or right-to-build (Hak Guna Bangunan) title, so banks and developers will ask for proof of separation before the loan is signed. That is what happened to the reader at the start of this article. The answer they got: it is possible, provided they make a marriage agreement, and since 2016 that agreement can be made after the wedding.

The practical advice is simple. If you plan to take out a mortgage in the first few years of marriage, ask your bank before you sign the deed, and take the answer to your notary. Separation does not have to be total. You can separate what you each brought in and a family business, while pooling what you earn during the marriage.

## Your partner's debts: what is actually protected

One question at Hukumonline: a husband borrowed Rp20 million from a friend without his wife knowing, and the friend threatened to take the wife's motorbike even though the husband had offered to pay in instalments. Hukumonline's answer: a personal debt taken on without the spouse's consent cannot be recovered from the spouse's own property, and someone who takes that property by force may face a theft charge.

So even without a prenup, the law already offers some protection. The problem is proof. Without an agreement, the line between joint and personal property is often blurred, especially for things bought after the wedding. A registered agreement puts that line in writing, where a creditor can be shown it. This matters most when one of you runs a business and borrows regularly for working capital.

## If you are already married

Postnuptial agreements are now valid, but there is a timing trap. Article 29(3) says the agreement takes effect from the date of the marriage, unless the agreement says otherwise. So you and your notary have to choose: does the agreement reach back to the wedding date, or does it take effect only from the day the deed is signed?

If it takes effect from signing, a house or car bought before that date stays joint property. If it reaches back, remember that Article 29(4) prohibits an agreement from harming third parties. A bank that already holds security over your house does not lose it because you signed a new deed. Go through your existing assets and debts in detail with the notary before you choose.

One more thing that often surprises people: a marriage agreement can only be registered against a marriage the state has recorded. Couples married in a religious ceremony only (nikah siri) must register the marriage first.

## Registering at the KUA or Dukcapil

The notarial deed binds the two of you from the moment it is signed. To bind banks, buyers and creditors, it has to be registered where your marriage was recorded. There is no need to go to court.

**Muslim couples (KUA).** The basis is a circular from the Director General of Islamic Guidance dated 28 September 2017. The marriage registrar notes the agreement in the remarks column of the marriage certificate and in the marriage book. Bring:

- ID cards (KTP) and family cards (Kartu Keluarga) for both of you.
- The notarial marriage agreement, legalised.
- Both the husband's and the wife's marriage books, if the agreement was made after the wedding.

**Non-Muslim couples (Dukcapil).** The basis is Director General of Population and Civil Registration circular 472.2/5876/DUKCAPIL, dated 19 May 2017. The officer adds a marginal note to the register and to your marriage certificate excerpt. Bring:

- Electronic ID cards and family cards.
- A copy of the notarial marriage agreement.
- The original marriage certificate excerpt.

The Director General of Dukcapil has stated that only agreements made as a notarial deed can be registered. A private agreement, even one signed over a duty stamp, will not be accepted.

## For Muslim couples: taklik talak and maintenance

The Compilation of Islamic Law (KHI) recognises two forms of marriage agreement. The first is taklik talak, the conditional statement the husband usually reads after the akad, printed in the marriage book. The second is any other agreement that does not conflict with Islamic law, including arrangements about property.

Article 47 of the KHI allows an agreement to merge or separate each spouse's personal property and earnings. Article 48, however, sets a firm limit: separating property cannot remove the husband's obligation to provide for the household. A husband who earns less still has to provide, and a wife who earns more is still entitled to that support, even with separate property. If the agreement is breached, Article 51 gives the wife the right to seek annulment or divorce at the Religious Court.

## Clauses that will not hold

Article 29(2) of the Marriage Law prohibits agreements that break the law, religion or public decency. The Civil Code adds other limits: a marriage agreement cannot reduce parental authority over children, and it cannot waive or rearrange the spouses' rights to inherit from their descendants.

In practice, the clauses couples ask for most often that will not stand up in court include deciding child custody in advance and penalties for infidelity. Hukumonline doubts that fidelity clauses fall within the scope of a marriage agreement at all. Custody is always decided by a judge on the child's interests.

On inheritance between husband and wife, the answer turns out to be less clear than people assume. A court has ruled that a total separation of property also removed the surviving spouse's right to inherit, and that question is now before the Constitutional Court. Read [lessons from a spouse's inheritance case](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tips-hubungan/perjanjian-pranikah-dan-hak-waris-pasangan) before you sign.

## Talking to your parents about it

In many families, "separate property" sounds like an accusation. The future son- or daughter-in-law can feel suspected of chasing money, and the other side's parents can feel their child is being treated like a stranger.

A few things usually help. The person who raises it with the parents should be their own child, not the future in-law. Give a concrete reason, such as "Papa wants to pass the shop shares to me, and the notary says it's cleaner if they're kept separate," rather than a general one about protection. If the agreement is actually your parents' request because of a family business, tell your partner from the start that it is a family condition, not doubt about them.

Avoid springing it in the last month before the wedding. If one of you feels pressured, the agreement can be challenged later, and the run-up to the wedding suffers too. A postnuptial agreement is still available if you need more time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an unregistered agreement still valid?

It is valid between husband and wife from the moment the deed is signed before a notary. But banks, buyers and creditors are not bound until it is registered at the KUA or Dukcapil.

### How long does registration take?

It depends on the office. The sources we read give different figures, from a few weeks to about two months for the whole process from notary to completed registration. If you need the agreement for a mortgage, start well before the loan signing date.

### Can the agreement be changed if circumstances change?

Yes, provided both of you agree, it is done by a new notarial deed, and it does not harm third parties. The change also needs to be registered, just like the original agreement.

### Does separate property mean each of us pays our own household costs?

Not necessarily. The agreement can set out who pays for what, and for Muslim couples the husband's duty to provide still applies whatever the agreement says.
