"The house is in my name, so it's my house." It sounds reasonable, and it is often wrong. In a marriage, the name on the certificate shows who is registered as the right holder. It does not automatically decide whether the house belongs to one spouse or to both.

This article explains how Indonesian law sees a married couple's home, and what happens at the three moments that most often raise the question: when the house is sold, on divorce, and when one spouse dies.

## What decides it: when the house was bought, and with what money

Article 35(1) of the Marriage Law says property acquired during the marriage becomes joint property. It says nothing about whose name the property is registered in. So, without a marital agreement:

- **A house bought during the marriage** is joint property, whether the certificate is in the wife's name, the husband's, or both.
- **A house bought before the marriage** is its owner's pre-marital property.
- **A house inherited by or gifted to one spouse** stays that spouse's property, even if received during the marriage.

There is a grey area that often ends in dispute: a house bought before the wedding with a mortgage, with the instalments then paid during the marriage from salaries. Some opinions count the instalments paid during the marriage as part of the joint property. There is no single answer, and this is where a marital agreement is most useful.

## When the house is sold or mortgaged

For joint property, a spouse may only act with the consent of both (Article 36(1)). For Muslim couples, KHI Article 92 confirms that joint property may not be sold or transferred without the other spouse's consent.

In practice, the land deed official (PPAT) will ask for your spouse's consent when you sell a house bought during the marriage, even if the certificate is in your name alone. A bank also asks for your spouse's signature when the house is used as collateral. If you want to be able to act alone, the house has to be personal property, and its status has to be provable.

## On divorce

Joint property is divided according to the law that applies to the couple (Article 37 of the Marriage Law). For Muslim couples, KHI Article 97 gives each spouse half, unless the marriage agreement says otherwise. The name on the certificate does not change that: a house bought during the marriage in the wife's name is still divided, and the husband is still entitled to his share.

Judges do not always split evenly. Some decisions weigh each spouse's contribution. But the starting point is still joint property, not the name on the certificate.

## When one spouse dies

This is the moment that most often surprises families. For Muslim couples, KHI Article 96 gives half of the joint property to the surviving spouse. The other half goes into the deceased's estate and is divided among all the heirs, including the surviving spouse, the children and, in some cases, the deceased's parents.

A simple example: a house bought during the marriage, certificate in the wife's name, and the husband dies. The wife does not automatically own the whole house. Half is hers as joint property, and the other half is the husband's estate, divided among his heirs. If the house is sold later, the heirs usually have to sign too.

For non-Muslim families, inheritance follows the law that applies to them, but the joint-property principle is the same. ## If you want the house to belong to one spouse only

Two things make a house's status clear:

1. **A registered marital agreement.** The agreement can provide that property acquired during the marriage stays with each spouse, or that a particular house belongs to one spouse. It can be made before the wedding or during the marriage. See our [guide to postnuptial agreements](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tips-hubungan/perjanjian-kawin-setelah-menikah).
2. **Evidence of where the money came from.** If the house was bought from pre-marital property or an inheritance, keep the trail: bank statements, the sale deed of the earlier property, the inheritance certificate.

For couples buying with a mortgage, separate property has its own effect on how the bank assesses you. Read [how separate property affects mortgages and debt](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tips-hubungan/perjanjian-pranikah-pisah-harta-kpr-dan-utang) before deciding.

## For Indonesian and foreign couples

For mixed couples, the question is not only who owns the house, but whether it may be owned at all. Without separate property, the Indonesian spouse cannot hold freehold (Hak Milik) or building rights (HGB), because the foreign spouse's share of joint property collides with the Basic Agrarian Law. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is it different if the certificate is in both our names?

Not for its status as joint property. Both names simply make the record clearer and ensure both of you take part in any transaction.

### The house was bought with the wife's business income during the marriage. Is it still joint property?

Without an agreement, yes. Business income earned during the marriage is joint property, whoever runs the business.

### Can a husband gift the house to his wife so it becomes hers?

Gifts between husband and wife during the marriage are restricted by law, and the tax consequences are not simple. A marital agreement is usually the cleaner route. Ask a notary or PPAT before choosing any method.

### Can a husband claim a house the wife inherited from her parents on divorce?

Not as joint property. An inheritance stays with the spouse who received it, provided it has not been mixed and its status can be proven.
