In many Javanese families there is one moment that almost always follows the introduction of a future son- or daughter-in-law. Someone, usually a mother or grandmother, asks lightly, "What day were they born? Which pasaran?" The question is rarely small talk. From the answer, the family works out weton jodoh: the couple's compatibility according to Javanese primbon.

This article explains how to work it out yourself, what each result means, why tables online so often differ, and how families usually respond to a result that is less than good.

## What weton is

Weton is the combination of the day of the week you were born on and the Javanese pasaran day. Pasaran is a five-day cycle: Legi, Pahing, Pon, Wage and Kliwon. Because the week has seven days and the pasaran five, the same combination comes round every 35 days. So a person is not simply born on a Friday, but on Friday Legi, Friday Pahing, and so on.

The best-known example is Proclamation Day. 17 August 1945 fell on a Friday Legi, and Soekarno himself is said to have remarked on it: "This Friday is Friday Legi."

## Neptu values for days and pasaran

Every day and every pasaran has a value called neptu. The values are the same in almost every primbon source.

| Day | Neptu | Pasaran | Neptu |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sunday | 5 | Legi | 5 |
| Monday | 4 | Pahing | 9 |
| Tuesday | 3 | Pon | 7 |
| Wednesday | 7 | Wage | 4 |
| Thursday | 8 | Kliwon | 8 |
| Friday | 6 | | |
| Saturday | 9 | | |

A person's neptu is the day's neptu plus the pasaran's. Friday Legi, for example, is worth 6 + 5 = 11. The lowest possible value is 7 (Tuesday Wage) and the highest 18 (Saturday Pahing).

## How to calculate weton jodoh

The most common method has three steps:

1. Work out your neptu and your partner's.
2. Add the two together.
3. Divide the total by 8 and look at the remainder.

The remainder gives the result:

| Remainder | Result | Meaning in primbon |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pegat | Prone to separation |
| 2 | Ratu | Respected, a household "made royal" |
| 3 | Jodoh | Destined to be together |
| 4 | Topo | Tested by trouble early on, easier later |
| 5 | Tinari | Happy and comfortably provided for |
| 6 | Padu | Frequent quarrels, though rarely ending in separation |
| 7 | Sujanan | Prone to infidelity |
| 0 | Pesthi | Peaceful and harmonious |

Sources do not agree on the best result. Some name Ratu, some Pesthi, others Jodoh. What is clear is that Pegat, Padu and Sujanan are the three results that usually make a family want to talk further.

## Example: Dimas and Sari

Dimas, a fictional groom, was born on 12 March 1996, a Tuesday Legi. His neptu is 3 + 5 = 8. Sari was born on 3 August 1997, a Sunday Kliwon. Her neptu is 5 + 8 = 13.

Together they make 8 + 13 = 21. Divided by 8 that is 2, remainder 5. A remainder of 5 means **Tinari**: happy and comfortably provided for.

To find the weton of your own date of birth, you can count back from a date whose weton is known. Remember that the pasaran repeats every five days. 17 August 1945 was a Friday Legi, so any date a multiple of five days away from it also falls on Legi. The more practical route is a Javanese calendar, which prints the pasaran on every date.

## Why tables online so often differ

If you try a few websites you will find different results for the same couple. There are several reasons.

**Two tables are equally popular.** Besides dividing by 8, many sites use a table that repeats every 9 numbers, in which totals of 1, 9, 10, 18, 19, 27, 28 and 36 read as Pegat. With that table, Dimas and Sari's total of 21 reads as **Jodoh**, not Tinari. Both are good results, but they have different names.

**There are other methods altogether.** Some primbon divide the total by 4, with the results gentho, gembili, sri and punggel. Others divide by 10 and read the remainder as wasesa segara, satriya wirang, bumi kapetak and so on, through to lebu katiup angin. Which method is used depends on the region and on who is doing the counting.

**There are miscalculations.** Some online articles contain tables with duplicated numbers or wrong division. When two sources contradict each other, the method your own family elder uses is the one to rely on.

## If the result is not a good one

Field studies in several parts of Java find that the weton calculation is usually done by a customary elder. How families respond to a less favourable result varies:

- **Seeking a remedy.** Some families hold a ruwatan or selamatan to ward off misfortune, or choose a special wedding day believed to neutralise the result.
- **Giving more in charity and prayer.** For a result such as Pegat, some advise giving to orphans and widows, along with effort and prayer.
- **Calling off the plans.** In a small number of families that hold to custom very strictly, certain results are still seen as a reason not to go ahead.
- **Not making an issue of it.** Many families now work out the weton as part of tradition, without letting the result decide anything.

On the religious side, a piece on NU Online explains that working out weton is permitted as long as one believes that only God determines fate, and becomes forbidden if the weton itself is believed to decide it. Its conclusion is that weton and the fate of a marriage have no absolute connection.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does weton jodoh have to be worked out before the lamaran?

There is no fixed rule. In families that follow primbon, it is often done before the formal proposal, so that if anything needs discussing there is still plenty of time.

### Our result is Pegat. Does that mean we can't marry?

No. A weton result is a prediction within a tradition, not a legal or religious prohibition. Many families choose a remedy and others do not make an issue of it. What matters is talking it through calmly with both families.

### Why does our family elder use a different method from this article?

Because there really are many methods. Primbon has been handed down region to region and teacher to student, so the divisors, tables and interpretations can all differ. If your family has its own method, that is usually the one used.

### Can weton be worked out for a partner who isn't Javanese?

Yes, since all it needs is a date of birth. In mixed couples it is usually done at the request of the Javanese side of the family.
