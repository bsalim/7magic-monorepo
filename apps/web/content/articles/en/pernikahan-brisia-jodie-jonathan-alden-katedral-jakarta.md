On Saturday night, 6 December 2025, the ballroom at Pondok Indah Golf had no pelaminan, the raised dais where Indonesian couples usually sit to receive guests. The room was dressed as a winter scene in blue, white and the sparkle of crystal. The couple's theme even had a name: "Dreamy of Aurora". That evening Brisia Jodie also sang "Makin Aku Cinta" for her guests.

Three days earlier, she and Jonathan Alden received a Catholic wedding blessing at Jakarta Cathedral. The day before the church, the family held a Javanese siraman.

Many Indonesian couples will recognise this mix. The religious ceremony is in church, the family still wants some adat, and the couple themselves picture a reception that feels modern. Brisia and Jonathan's wedding shows all of it can fit into one week without anything being squeezed out.

## How the week ran

- **Wednesday, 8 January 2025**: the proposal is announced. It had taken place in Switzerland.
- **Saturday, 21 June 2025**: the engagement ceremony.
- **Tuesday, 2 December 2025**: a Javanese siraman.
- **Wednesday, 3 December 2025**: the Catholic blessing at Jakarta Cathedral.
- **Saturday, 6 December 2025**: the reception at the Pondok Indah Golf Ballroom, Jakarta.

From engagement to church blessing took about five and a half months. For a couple booking a major church and a Jakarta ballroom, that's a tight window, so it pays to lock the dates in early.

Notice too that the proposal and the engagement were two separate events. The proposal happened in Switzerland, while the engagement was held as an event of its own in June. Keeping them apart lets you design the proposal entirely to your own taste, while your parents still get the family gathering they hope for. If the two families have never met formally, the engagement is also the right place to start talking about dates, which adat to follow, and who pays for what.

## A Javanese siraman before church

The siraman is a ritual bathing of the bride or groom-to-be, a symbolic cleansing before marriage. In the Javanese order of events it is usually held the day before the akad or the church blessing. Brisia and Jonathan followed that order: siraman on Tuesday, church on Wednesday.

For families marrying their children in church, the siraman is often where adat stays alive. It takes place at home or at a family venue, the circle is small, and the parents have a clear role. If your family wants a Javanese element but the main ceremony is in church, the siraman is the easiest way in. Ask your parents which parts matter to them, then build the event from there.

## Three days between church and reception

The blessing was on a Wednesday and the reception on a Saturday. The gap looks minor, but it helps a lot.

First, the couple don't have to switch from church to ballroom within a few hours. Second, the guest list can be split. A weekday blessing tends to draw family and the closest friends, while a weekend reception takes in the wider circle. The decorators also get time to build the stage without racing the clock.

If your budget doesn't stretch to two big days, the principle still works. A morning blessing with a small group, a family lunch, then a reception on another day at whatever scale you can manage.

## A reception without a dais: "Dreamy of Aurora"

The easiest part of this wedding to borrow is the reception. There was no traditional pelaminan. Instead the room was styled as a winter wonderland in blue, white and crystal.

Naming a theme sounds like a flourish, but it's useful. A single phrase like "Dreamy of Aurora" lets you brief the decorator, florist, invitation designer and make-up artist all at once. Every vendor holds the same picture, and you get a simple test for each proposal: does it fit the theme or not.

Dropping the dais also changes how guests move. With no raised stage where the couple sits for hours, you're free to greet guests table by table, and the handshake queue stops being the centre of the evening. Just make sure the wider family knows early, because to some parents a pelaminan is still non-negotiable.

## A gown inspired by Kate Middleton

Brisia's gown was designed by Diana M Putri, inspired by Kate Middleton. This approach is worth copying. Rather than turning up at the designer's with dozens of screenshots that contradict each other, name one person whose style you love. The designer will grasp the general direction straight away and then adapt it to your figure and the mood of your day.

## Five bridesmaids and a song from the bride

Brisia had five bridesmaids: Lyodra Ginting, Nabila Taqiyyah, Maria Simorangkir, Anggi Marito and Anggis Devaki.

Five is a number that's still easy to manage. Outfits can be matched without fuss, fitting schedules aren't too hard to line up, and each bridesmaid can be given a clear job on the day. If you'd like to involve more friends, think about other roles such as welcoming guests or leading a prayer, so the bridesmaid group stays small.

Then came the moment when Brisia herself sang "Makin Aku Cinta" at her reception. For a singer, that's natural. If you aren't one, you can still borrow the idea in another form: a short letter read aloud, a song sung with friends, or a few words of thanks to your parents. Guests tend to remember best the parts that come straight from the couple.

## What you can borrow

- Separate the religious ceremony and the reception by a few days if you can. Your energy and your vendors' work both benefit.
- Use the siraman as the family's space for adat when the main ceremony is in church.
- Give your reception theme a name, then share that phrase with every vendor as the reference point.
- If you choose a reception without a dais, tell your parents and the wider family well ahead.
- Give your designer one clear style reference, not a pile of random photos.
- Plan one moment delivered by the couple themselves, however small.
