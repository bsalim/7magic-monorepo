Most Singapore prewedding albums end up in the same place: Marina Bay Sands behind you, a Supertree beside you. The photos are lovely, but you have already seen them in hundreds of other feeds. Yet the same city has a Majapahit-style gateway on a hilltop, and a former police station with 927 windows painted in rainbow colours.

We put this list together for couples flying in from Indonesia just for the shoot, and also for couples who live in Singapore and want a backdrop that looks different from their friends' albums. For each location we describe what the photos look like, the best time to go, how to get there, and who to contact about permits. We checked the rules and fees below against each operator's official pages on 7 October 2026.

## Rules that apply everywhere

### Permits: who handles what

Singapore splits photo shoots into two kinds: personal, which generally needs no permit, and commercial, which does. The trouble is that most agencies do not say which side a prewedding shoot with a paid photographer falls on. Only three places on this list answer that for themselves. Gardens by the Bay says its photo packages are "strictly for personal filming or photography sessions such as wedding, graduation, maternity or family shoots". Jewel Changi Airport requires an application for every wedding shoot as a category of its own, separate from commercial. National Gallery Singapore sells dedicated wedding photoshoot slots. For every other location, ask the agency in writing before you pay a photographer or buy a plane ticket.

- **Parks and nature reserves (NParks).** According to NParks, a permit is required "for all filming and commercial photography in our parks and nature reserves". There is no application fee, and each application covers one location. Note the words *all filming*: if you are also recording a prewedding video, that sentence does not exempt it.
- **NParks' lead time is not consistent.** NParks' filming permit page (updated 2 September 2026) asks for applications at least one month ahead, while its photography page says two weeks. Confirm with NParks before you lock in a date.
- **NParks' small-scale permit** covers fewer than 16 people, with no drones and no bulky equipment such as dolly tracks. Its conditions: shoot on weekdays at off-peak times, with no flash, no props, confetti or flower petals, and no equipment other than a tripod. It does not cover nature reserves, nature parks, Fort Canning Park, Singapore Botanic Gardens and a number of other city parks, which have their own forms.
- **Streets and walkways (IMDA).** IMDA's guide says permits are generally not required for filming on Singapore's streets and walkways, as long as you do not obstruct vehicles or pedestrians. To use someone's property, ask the owner, and as a courtesy tell neighbours or nearby businesses at least 3 working days ahead. Common areas in HDB estates fall under the Town Councils.
- **PUB sites, including Marina Barrage.** Commercial filming and photography need a permit or prior approval from PUB, and its form only allows shoots from 7am to 7pm.
- **Sentosa (SDC).** Contact Sentosa Development Corporation at least 10 working days ahead for any filming or photography request.

### Drones

Plan your shoot without a drone. To fly outdoors for business, CAAS (Singapore's civil aviation authority) requires an Operator Permit and a Class 1 Activity Permit, whatever the drone weighs, plus a UA Pilot Licence. CAAS does not mention weddings specifically; treating a hired photographer as business is our reading. As of October 2026, the processing fees are S$700 for the Operator Permit (first UA type) plus $200 for the CFMS subscription, then S$120 per Class 1 Activity Permit for one activity or one block of dates. Operator Permit holders must also fit a CFMS tracker, listed at S$265 "(subject to prevailing GST)". Average processing takes 10 working days for an Operator Permit and 5 working days for an Activity Permit.

Anywhere within 5 km of an aerodrome is a no-fly zone. Many locations also have their own bans: Gardens by the Bay bans drones in all three of its gardens, Singapore Botanic Gardens bans them, Sentosa bans recreational drones, and NParks' small-scale permit excludes drones. Jewel lies within 5 km of Changi Airport.

### Weather

Rain in Singapore falls more often in the afternoon, so mornings usually stay drier. The Meteorological Service Singapore (MSS) names two exceptions. During the Southwest Monsoon (June to September), occasional "Sumatra Squalls" bring 40-80 km/h gusts between the predawn hours and midday. In the early Northeast Monsoon (usually December to early January), rain can fall continuously and is not tied to the afternoon.

Rainfall is heaviest from November to January. The inter-monsoon periods, late March to May and October to November, bring thunderstorms in the afternoon and early evening, and MSS calls October to November generally wetter. Humidity is often above 90% before sunrise and drops to about 60% on dry afternoons, so tell your hair and make-up team early. If the sky turns against you, three locations on this list are indoors: Gardens by the Bay, Jewel and National Gallery Singapore.

### Getting around by MRT

Almost every location can be reached from an MRT station. Pulau Ubin needs a bus and a boat, and Tanjong Beach needs the Sentosa Express. The station distances we give are straight-line distances we calculated from the Singapore Land Authority's OneMap, not walking routes, so use them only to pick the nearest station. One note: on LTA's latest official map (July 2026), Bayfront station is now coded CC34/DT16, although some older pages still say CE1.

## 1. Fort Canning Park: a Majapahit-style gateway in the city centre

In Sang Nila Utama Garden, one of nine historical gardens in Fort Canning Park, you will find a recreated 14th-century Southeast Asian palace garden: a symmetrical layout, a reflective pool, and a series of gateways that NParks calls "Javanese split gates". Indonesian eyes recognise them at once as *candi bentar*, a gateway style that traces its roots to the Majapahit Empire. The garden is named after Sang Nila Utama, the Palembang prince who founded early Singapore. Standing together in the gap of a gate with the pool in front gives you a symmetrical frame that feels close to home.

On the edge of the park is the Tree Tunnel, a spiral staircase where, photographed from the bottom, foliage frames a circular opening to the sky. It is popular and often has a queue.

**Best time:** the park is open 24 hours. For the Tree Tunnel, arrive as early as you can, ideally on a weekday.

**Getting there:** Fort Canning station (DT20) is closest, about 373 m away. NParks also lists Clarke Quay and Dhoby Ghaut as nearby stations.

**Permit:** Fort Canning Park is excluded from NParks' small-scale permit, so commercial shoots use the "other filming and commercial photography" form. On the same park page, however, NParks also asks for a Park Use Request form for photography and filming. Contact NParks and ask which form applies to your shoot. There is no application fee, and the conditions of no flash, no props or confetti, and tripod only still apply. For the Tree Tunnel, NParks directs all requests to LTA (the Land Transport Authority). We could not confirm LTA's rules or fees, so ask LTA directly before you book.

## 2. National Gallery Singapore: colonial corridors, indoors

The Gallery occupies two national monuments, the former Supreme Court and the former City Hall, now joined by bridges and a new roof canopy. You get long colonnaded corridors in the colonial style and domed spaces, without having to worry about rain. Bookable areas include the City Hall foyer and corridors, the Padang Atrium, the Padang Deck, the Supreme Court foyers (Levels 1 and 3), the Supreme Court Terrace, and the Lower and Upper Link Bridges.

**Best time:** weekday slots run 7.30-9.30am (before the Gallery opens), 10.30am-12.30pm and 4-6pm. Weekends have the 7.30-9.30am slot only. The Link Bridges can only be used in the pre-opening slot, so choose it if the bridges are on your shot list. There are no sessions on the eves of public holidays, on public holidays, or over the F1 weekend.

**Getting there:** City Hall station (NS25/EW13), about 274 m away. Esplanade (CC3) is also close.

**Permit and fees:** the wedding personal photoshoot costs **S$381.50 per group of 5 people, including GST** (as of October 2026). Up to 2 groups per slot, booked at least 10 working days ahead. Gallery Insider members get 15% off. No artworks may appear in the frame, for copyright reasons, and only handheld equipment is allowed. Commercial shoots have their own hours (7.30pm-9.30am), are applied for 14 working days ahead, are accompanied by security staff, and are priced on enquiry.

## 3. Old Hill Street Police Station: 927 rainbow windows

This building at 140 Hill Street, on the corner of Hill Street and River Valley Road, has 927 windows painted in rainbow colours. The first four storeys share one colour intensity; higher up, the colours grow stronger to accentuate the cantilevered balconies. Built in 1934 as a police station and barracks, it was gazetted a National Monument on 18 December 1998, and today it houses MDDI (the Ministry of Digital Development and Information) and MCCY (the Ministry of Culture, Community and Youth). Ask your photographer to step well back so the rows of coloured windows fill the background behind you both.

**Best time:** because you photograph the facade from the pavement, the building's opening hours do not matter. As with other outdoor locations, mornings are usually safer from rain.

**Getting there:** Clarke Quay (NE5) about 458 m, Fort Canning (DT20) about 470 m, and City Hall about 524 m.

**Permit:** from the public pavement, the IMDA rule applies: no permit is needed as long as you obstruct no one. To use the building's premises, MDDI asks you to write in for permission. MDDI also states that images of the building can generally be used for educational purposes but not for commercial use, so if your studio wants to use the photos in its own promotion, ask first. We could not confirm a fee; ask MDDI.

## 4. Gardens by the Bay: the Flower Dome or Cloud Forest before opening

The Flower Dome and Cloud Forest are two cooled conservatories. To make your photos look different, book the exclusive one-hour session before opening, while the domes are still empty of visitors. The Flower Dome is a Mediterranean-climate glasshouse kept at 23-25°C with a changing flower field; its operator says the Flower Dome holds the Guinness World Record for the largest glass greenhouse. The Cloud Forest has a 35 m indoor waterfall and a plant-clad mountain with aerial walkways.

Check what exhibitions are on, because their decor ends up in the frame. The Cloud Forest currently hosts *Jurassic World: The Experience* with animatronic dinosaurs. Floral Fantasy has the Disney Garden of Wonder exhibition from 8 June 2026 to 14 March 2027, and tripods are banned there. The Flower Dome hosts "Sunflower Surprise: Garden by the Ocean", a Portugal-themed sunflower display, from 9 October to 9 November 2026.

**Best time:** the pre-opening slot for the Flower Dome and Cloud Forest is 8-9am, and for Floral Fantasy 9-10am. The operator strongly encourages wedding couples to shoot on weekdays or book a session. Tripods are not allowed on weekends and public holidays. Both domes open 9am-9pm. Note the next maintenance closures: the Cloud Forest on 26 October 2026 and the Flower Dome on 16 November 2026.

**Getting there:** Gardens by the Bay station (TE22), Exit 1. You can also come from Bayfront (CC34/DT16), Exit B, and cross via the Dragonfly Bridge or the Meadow Bridge.

**Permit and fees (as of October 2026):**

- Personal Photoshoot package **S$188 nett**, including 4 adult tickets to any two of the Flower Dome, Cloud Forest, Floral Fantasy or Supertree Observatory, a two-way buggy transfer, and 2 hours of free parking.
- Cloud Forest sessions carry a compulsory S$56 nett surcharge because of the Jurassic World exhibition, in place since 29 May 2025.
- A third attraction adds S$70, "inclusive of 1 additional hour, 4 admission tickets and buggy services". The official page does not say whether this figure is nett.
- Exclusive pre-opening session: S$697.60 Monday to Friday and S$1,308 on weekends and public holidays, per one-hour session at one location, including the buggy service. This is a "special opening rate" promotion after a 20% discount, so it may change, and the page does not say whether GST is included. There are no refunds once confirmed.
- Friends of the Gardens members (Flower Dome, Cloud Forest & OCBC Skyway memberships) get 20% off.
- Book by email at weddings@gardensbythebay.com.sg. Drones are banned, and commercial shoots need approval applied for at least 14 days ahead.

## 5. Jewel Changi Airport: for couples who have just landed

If you are flying in from Indonesia, this can be your first shoot in Singapore. Jewel connects directly to Terminal 1, and by link bridge to Terminals 2 and 3. At its centre is the HSBC Rain Vortex, a 40 m waterfall billed as the world's tallest indoor waterfall, surrounded by the terraced Forest Valley. On Level 5, Canopy Park has a hedge maze, a mirror maze and the Canopy Bridge.

**Best time:** Canopy Park wedding package slots run Monday to Thursday only (excluding school holidays, public holidays and their eves), 10am-12pm or 7-9pm, with last admission 30 minutes before closing. The Rain Vortex runs 10am-10pm daily. The Light & Sound show is on Monday to Thursday at 8pm and 9pm, and Friday to Sunday, public holidays and their eves at 8pm, 9pm and 10pm. Your evening package slot overlaps the 8pm show.

**Getting there:** Changi Airport station (CG2), about 343 m away.

**Permit and fees:** Jewel requires an application for every wedding photo and video shoot, via contact.us@jewelchangiairport.com. The Canopy Park wedding package is **S$188 before GST** (as of October 2026), for the couple plus up to 3 crew, valid at all attractions except the Bouncing Net and Walking Net. A Jewel Suite add-on (1 hour) and a solemnisation package are available, but their prices are not listed; ask venuehire@jewelchangiairport.com. The link bridges from Terminals 2 and 3 are Changi Airport premises, not Jewel's, so requests for them go to filmphoto@changiairport.com. The request form linked from Jewel's FAQs (version of 22 April 2026) asks for submission at least 5 working days ahead, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays. That form does not cover paid attractions, so ask whether the same lead time applies to the Canopy Park package. The terms on the same form do not allow wedding photography on the East and West Trails of the Forest Valley, inside tenanted premises, or in any ticketed attraction, Canopy Park included (for Canopy Park, use the package above), and ask you not to occupy any shared public space for more than 30 minutes. A drone needs a CAAS permit as well as a Jewel filming permit.

## 6. Kampong Gelam: the Sultan Mosque from Bussorah Street, and Haji Lane

URA and the National Heritage Board spell it Kampong Glam, while the Singapore Tourism Board now writes Kampong Gelam. Both are correct. The two golden onion domes of the Sultan Mosque (3 Muscat Street) frame best from Bussorah Street, a short pedestrian street lined with restaurants and shops. Around the base of each dome runs a belt of glass bottles donated by the poor. Haji Lane is a narrow lane of shophouses with independent shops and cafes. The Singapore Tourism Board's official map points to its pop-art murals, but murals change, so check before the day.

This district is close to our own history. Kampong Gelam was once home to Javanese, Sumatran, Baweanese and Banjarese communities, and Haji Lane once hosted pilgrims on their way to the hajj. The present Sultan Mosque was rebuilt and completed in 1932 to a design by Denis Santry of Swan & Maclaren, and was gazetted a national monument in 1975.

**Best time:** the mosque takes walk-in visits Saturday to Thursday, 10am-12pm and 2-4pm, and is closed to visitors on Friday. Avoid prayer times; the mosque office is also closed then.

**Getting there:** Bugis station (EW12/DT14). Haji Lane is about 270-367 m from Bugis in a straight line, depending on the platform. The mosque gives Bugis and Jalan Besar (DT22) as about 10 minutes' walk each.

**Permit and etiquette:** no official page covers prewedding shoots at the mosque. Treat the mosque as a backdrop you photograph from the public street, where the IMDA rule applies, and do not stage a gown shoot in the mosque compound without written approval. If you go in as visitors, use Gate 5, which faces Bussorah Street. Men wear sleeved tops and long pants. Women wear long-sleeved tops with no cleavage and bottoms to the ankle, and nothing see-through; robes are lent at the counter. Remove your footwear, keep silent, and enter the prayer halls only with a mosque official. The mosque's own rule: "Photography without flash is allowed. Approval is required for video recording." Haji Lane is a public street, but the shopfronts belong to the businesses, so ask before using a doorway or shop interior as a set.

## 7. Koon Seng Road (Joo Chiat): pastel Peranakan houses

This road in the Joo Chiat Conservation Area is popular with tourists and Instagrammers, and the Singapore Tourism Board's own map says "Pose in front of the prettiest street in Singapore". Two- and three-storey terrace houses stand in a row, with colourful, ornate facades in Peranakan or Chinese styles. URA calls them "some of the finest examples of Straits Eclectic architecture" in the area. Look at the details for close-ups: bat-shaped vents on the first storey (Chinese, for good luck), timber fretwork fascia boards (Malay), and plaster dentils and floral swags (Western). Most of the houses were built in the 1920s and 1930s, and the area was gazetted for conservation in 1991.

**Best time:** these houses are lived in, so choose a reasonable hour and keep the shoot quiet. Mornings are usually drier too.

**Getting there:** about 1 km in a straight line from either Marine Parade (TE26, 934 m) or Eunos (EW7, 982 m). It is not a short walk, especially once you are in your gown, so consider a taxi.

**Permit:** no permit is needed as long as you stay on the public road and do not block traffic. Steps and doors belong to the residents: do not climb onto the steps or the five-foot way (the covered walkway in front), lean on gates, or open doors. Using the property needs the owner's permission, and IMDA recommends telling neighbours beforehand.

## 8. Emerald Hill: Peranakan terraces behind Orchard Road

A block off Orchard Road, with Peranakan Place at its mouth, Emerald Hill is lined with terrace houses in Transitional to Art-Deco styles. Many have a *pintu pagar*, which Roots (National Heritage Board) describes as "fence-like pairs of revolving doors", and ground-floor facades decorated with ceramic tilework. Their plaster and wood carving produce what URA calls "Chinese Baroque". The area was built up from about 1901 for wealthy Chinese and Straits Chinese families, and URA announced it as Singapore's first conservation area in 1985. An ornate pintu pagar with colourful tiles around it is enough to fill a portrait frame.

**Best time:** URA describes Emerald Hill as a quiet residential area, with commercial units only near Orchard Road. Come in the morning with a small team.

**Getting there:** Somerset (NS23), about 417 m. Dhoby Ghaut is about 934 m.

**Permit:** it is a public street, so the same IMDA rules as Koon Seng Road apply, including those on private property and telling the neighbours.

## 9. Tiong Bahru: 1930s Streamline Moderne flats

This is where the Singapore Improvement Trust (SIT) built Singapore's first public housing estate. Its architect, Alfred G. Church, designed these flats between 1936 and 1941 in the Streamline Moderne style, a late development of Art Deco, which gives you curved building corners and cantilevered shades. In 2003, URA gazetted 20 pre-war blocks for conservation. For couples who love the feel of old films, the curved lines of these low-rise blocks give a retro backdrop without extra props.

**Best time:** a weekday morning, with as small a team as possible, because this is a residential area.

**Getting there:** Havelock (TE16) is about 440 m from Tiong Bahru Market, closer than Tiong Bahru station (EW17) at about 597 m.

**Permit:** these blocks are people's homes. Photograph the facades from public paths (the IMDA rule), and do not go into corridors or stairwells without the managing body's consent. IMDA notes that common areas in HDB estates fall under the Town Councils, but we could not confirm whether these SIT blocks are now managed by a Town Council or privately, so ask first if you want to go inside.

## 10. Singapore Botanic Gardens: the Bandstand and Swan Lake

In NParks' own words, the Gardens are "Singapore's first and only UNESCO World Heritage Site". Two spots that suit a prewedding shoot are the Bandstand, which is also listed as a solemnisation venue, and Swan Lake. The setting is green and classic, far from the towers of Marina Bay. If you are also considering a solemnisation here, note that the Bandstand is unavailable for solemnisations from 10 November 2026 to 10 January 2027.

**Best time:** the Gardens open from 5am to midnight daily, with free entry except to the National Orchid Garden. Opening that early gives you time to shoot before the afternoon rain.

**Getting there:** Botanic Gardens station (CC19/DT9) for the Bukit Timah entrance, or Napier (TE12) for the Tanglin entrance.

**Permit:** the Gardens have their own rules and are excluded from NParks' small-scale permit. Casual photography sessions "such as family portraits and graduations" need no submission, but whether a prewedding shoot counts as casual is not stated, so ask NParks. If you need a permit, apply at least 14 days ahead; no fee is listed. Permitted equipment: a hand-held camera, a tripod, a boom mic, a monitor and hand-held reflectors. Props, backgrounds and decoration need approval. Without NParks' approval, no shooting is allowed at the 7 major entrance areas and their surroundings (including Palm Court), in the Rainforest, Mingxin Foundation Rambler's Ridge, OCBC Arboretum, COMO Adventure Grove, Jacob Ballas Children's Garden, at the Heritage Museum and its galleries, the Shaw Foundation Symphony Stage, office buildings, car parks, shops and F&B outlets, or in "Shelters and all sheltered areas within the Gardens". Whether the Bandstand counts as a sheltered area is unclear, so ask NParks before you build it into your plan. Drones are banned unless you have approval from both CAAS and NParks.

## 11. Marina Barrage: the skyline from the other side

Marina Barrage, at 8 Marina Gardens Drive, has a public sloping green roof with views of the Singapore skyline across Marina Reservoir. People come here to fly kites and have picnics. It is the alternative to the usual Marina Bay Sands frame: wide grass and open sky leave plenty of empty space around the two of you, which suits you if you like the [negative space prewedding](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/photography/prewedding-negative-space-minimalis) style.

**Best time:** the public areas and the Green Roof are open 24 hours, but PUB's form only allows shoots between 7am and 7pm. That means night skyline shots may not be approved if your shoot is treated as commercial, so plan within that window.

**Getting there:** Gardens by the Bay station (TE22), about 370 m away. SBS bus 400 also runs here.

**Permit:** PUB requires a permit for commercial filming and photography on its premises, with no fee stated. Tracks, cranes and dollies are not allowed. PUB has not said whether a prewedding shoot counts as commercial, so ask in writing. A drone needs a CAAS permit first.

## 12. Henderson Waves: a wooden wave bridge lit at night

Singapore's tallest pedestrian bridge is 274 m long and crosses Henderson Road, in Telok Blangah Hill Park on the Southern Ridges. Its deck is wood, and its seven undulating "ribs" form sheltered alcoves that make a natural frame for a photo of the two of you. From 7pm to 2am, the bridge is lit with LEDs. The bridge suits blue hour and night shoots, and NParks itself suggests coming in the evening to see it lit.

**Best time:** arrive shortly before 7pm so you can shoot against the dusk sky, then carry on once the lights come on. Because flash is not allowed under an NParks permit, your photographer will need to work with the bridge's LED light and a fast lens. The park is open 24 hours, but do not enter the Earth Trail after 7pm.

**Getting there:** Telok Blangah (CC28), about 868 m in a straight line, and the walk is uphill. NParks' Southern Ridges walk starts from HarbourFront (NE1/CC29) via Mount Faber.

**Permit:** Telok Blangah Hill Park is not on the exclusion list, so NParks' small-scale permit applies to commercial shoots: no fee, weekdays at off-peak times, no flash, props or confetti, and tripod only. NParks has not said whether your prewedding shoot needs it, nor settled the lead time (one month or two weeks), so confirm directly with NParks.

## 13. Pulau Ubin: wooden kampong houses and the Chek Jawa boardwalk

This island off the north-eastern corner of mainland Singapore has wooden kampong houses, jetties, and abandoned granite quarries. At its eastern end, Chek Jawa Wetlands has coastal and mangrove boardwalks where several ecosystems meet.

Check the current closures first. A section of Chek Jawa's Coastal Loop has been closed for maintenance since 15 September 2026, with completion expected on 30 April 2027, while the Mangrove Loop and the rest of the Coastal Loop stay open. The Chek Jawa Wetlands Tour is suspended, and a separate track closure from Chek Jawa is in force "until further notice".

**Best time:** Chek Jawa (the boardwalks, Jejawi Tower and visitor centre) is open 7am-7pm daily. Bumboats run from about 6am to 7pm, so plan to head back before the afternoon is over.

**Getting there:** there is no MRT on the island. Take a bumboat from Changi Point Ferry Terminal (51 Lorong Bekukong). To reach the terminal, take bus 59 from Tampines East (DT33), bus 29 from Tampines (EW2/DT32), or bus 2 from Tanah Merah (EW4). No station is within walking distance of the terminal. Bumboats are privately run and leave once 12 passengers are aboard. According to NParks, the 10-minute trip "typically costs $4 per person each way, with an additional $2 for bicycles". Outside those hours or with fewer passengers it costs more, and NParks asks you to confirm fares before each departure.

**Permit:** NParks requires a permit for all filming and commercial photography in its parks and nature reserves, and it has not said whether your prewedding shoot falls under that, so ask first. Shoots in nature reserves and nature parks are reviewed "stringently" and case by case, and whether Pulau Ubin or Chek Jawa falls in that category for permit purposes has not been stated. Apply well ahead and be ready for questions. Some villagers still live on the island, so ask before photographing anyone's house.

## 14. Tanjong Beach, Sentosa: the quiet beach at dusk

Sentosa describes this beach as a favourite with guests "who treasure its tranquility and solitude", and "especially romantic by night, with the twinkling lights of nearby ships". For a prewedding shoot, picture sand and sea with ship lights in the distance as the day fades.

**Best time:** dusk into early evening, when the ship lights start to show.

**Getting there:** from HarbourFront (NE1/CC29), go into VivoCity and take the Sentosa Express to Beach Station, then the Beach Shuttle to Tanjong Beach.

**Permit:** contact SDC at least 10 working days ahead at communications@sentosa.gov.sg. SDC has not stated a fee, or whether personal shoots need approval, so ask both at once. Recreational drones are banned on Sentosa.

## Checklist before you book

- Match your date against closures and exhibitions at your chosen locations, especially Gardens by the Bay and Chek Jawa.
- For NParks locations, ask NParks to confirm the lead time (one month or two weeks) and whether your shoot counts as commercial.
- Send written questions to the agencies that have not addressed prewedding shoots: NParks, Singapore Botanic Gardens, PUB, SDC, MDDI, and LTA for the Tree Tunnel.
- Keep their reply emails on your phone and show them if staff ask during the shoot.
- Book paid slots at Gardens by the Bay, National Gallery or Jewel once your date is fixed, because Gardens by the Bay gives no refunds once confirmed.
- Plan without a drone unless your photographer already holds CAAS permits and the site owner's consent.
- Put outdoor locations in the morning, and keep one indoor location as a rain backup.
- If the Sultan Mosque is in your plan, avoid Fridays and prayer times, and bring clothes that meet the mosque's dress code.
