The idea often comes from the kids. There's a group chat without the parents in it, and someone writes, "Mum and Dad hit 25 years next year. What if we take them to Bali and they say their vows again?" Then come the practical questions. Does a ceremony like that need registering? Who leads it? Can it be at a temple, on a beach or in a chapel?

This article answers those questions for Indonesian couples and for couples from overseas who want to renew their vows in Bali.

## What a Bali vow renewal is

A vow renewal is a ceremony in which a couple who are already married say their vows again. The reasons vary. Some are marking a 10th or 25th anniversary. Some had a very simple wedding, perhaps at the religious affairs office on a weekday or at a registry office abroad, and want to celebrate it with their children. Others have come through a hard stretch and want a fresh start.

The format is open. It can be just the two of you and a photographer, or a dinner with three generations of the family. What sets it apart from a wedding is its status.

## Is a vow renewal legal? It's symbolic

Under Article 2 of Indonesia's Marriage Law (Law No. 1 of 1974), a marriage is valid when performed according to the couple's religion and beliefs, and every marriage is recorded. Indonesia's civil registry records the vital events listed in Law No. 24 of 2013: births, deaths, stillbirths, marriages, divorces, acknowledgement of a child, legitimation of a child, adoption, change of name and change of citizenship. A vow renewal isn't one of them. Your marriage stays recorded on your original marriage certificate, and a ceremony in Bali doesn't produce a new one.

It's the same elsewhere. The Celebrants Network in Australia explains that couples can't be legally married a second time, even to each other, so the ceremony is a non-legal renewal of vows. West Northamptonshire Council in the UK says a renewal of vows has no standing in law. Two guests can sign a certificate as witnesses, but that certificate is commemorative.

Two things to watch for:

- **If your marriage was never registered**, a vow renewal won't fix it. Think of a wedding abroad that was never reported back home, or a religious marriage that was never recorded. That's sorted out at the civil registry or the religious affairs office, not through a ceremony.
- **For Muslim couples, a vow renewal is different from tajdid nikah.** Tajdid nikah is a renewal of the marriage contract practised in some communities, sometimes out of doubt about the first contract, sometimes as tradition. A 2026 study at the religious affairs office in Sumowono, Semarang Regency, concluded that it is acceptable under Islamic law as long as the pillars and conditions of marriage are met, it isn't believed to be a religious obligation, and it doesn't invalidate the first contract. If you're considering a renewed contract, talk to the religious affairs office or a scholar you trust, because that goes beyond a symbolic ceremony.

## Bali vow renewal ceremony styles

### A Balinese blessing, or melukat

Melukat is a self-purification tradition in Balinese Hindu religious life, using tirta, holy water, to cleanse body and mind. The ceremony follows the guidance of a pemangku or sulinggih (Balinese priests), and the details differ by village, temple and purpose. Because it's a religious practice, Indonesia.travel asks visitors not to treat melukat as just an attraction, to follow the dress rules of the sacred site, and not to photograph the parts that shouldn't be photographed.

Melukat can open the day of your vow renewal, followed by a symbolic ceremony somewhere else. If you're not Hindu, ask the pemangku first whether you may take part and how.

### On the beach or a clifftop

This is the style most people picture: the two of you facing the ocean, led by a celebrant, with family seated behind. There's plenty of choice, from the Uluwatu cliffs to the beaches of Nusa Dua. ### In a chapel

Several hotels and resorts in Bali have chapels, including glass chapels looking out over the sea or a garden. This style suits Christian couples who want their renewal with prayer and a blessing. Ask the chapel whether they can provide a minister, or whether you can bring your own. ## Who leads the ceremony

Because a vow renewal has no legal effect, there's no formal rule on who leads it. It depends on the style. For a beach or garden ceremony, it's usually a celebrant. For a Balinese blessing, a pemangku. For a chapel, a minister. Plenty of families also choose someone close to them, such as the eldest child or an old friend who was a witness at the first wedding.

Whoever you choose, tell them from the start that this is a vow renewal, so the wording and any certificate aren't made to look like a wedding.

## When to go to Bali

According to BMKG, Indonesia's weather agency, the 2026/2027 wet season starts in parts of Bali in November 2026 and across most of the island in December 2026, peaking in January and February 2027. BMKG's Bali climatology station expects it to last until April 2027. You don't have to avoid the wet season, but an outdoor ceremony needs a covered backup. If your anniversary falls in those months, consider a morning ceremony and a spot with indoor space close by.

Two dates in early 2027 are worth noting. Nyepi, the Balinese Day of Silence, falls on Monday 8 March 2027, and on Nyepi Ngurah Rai Airport stops operating for 24 hours, as it did for Nyepi 2026. Indonesia's joint ministerial decree lists Eid al-Fitr 1448 H on 10 and 11 March 2027, with collective leave days around it, so travel to Bali that week will be busy. The exact Eid date still awaits the Minister of Religious Affairs' decision after the moon-sighting session. ## Putting the plan together

The order that usually runs most smoothly:

1. **Decide who's coming.** Just the two of you, close family, or three generations. Numbers decide the venue and the accommodation.
2. **Choose the ceremony style.** A Balinese blessing, a beach or clifftop, or a chapel. That decides who leads it and when you need to contact them.
3. **Lock in the date, venue and accommodation.** In the wet season, ask about the backup plan before paying a deposit.
4. **Prepare the vows you'll say.** This is the most personal part. You might write new vows that mention what has happened since the first wedding.
5. **Sort out everything around it.** Outfits, a photographer, a family dinner and airport transfers. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Bali wedding renewal legally recorded?

No. A vow renewal isn't one of the vital events Indonesia's civil registry records, and it doesn't produce a new certificate. Your marriage stays recorded on your original certificate.

### How is it different from an elopement?

An elopement is a deliberately tiny wedding, and it can be legal if the religious ceremony is held and the marriage is registered. A vow renewal is for couples who are already married.
