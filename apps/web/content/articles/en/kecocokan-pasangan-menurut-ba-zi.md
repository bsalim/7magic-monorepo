In many Chinese Indonesian families, once a couple announces they plan to marry, grandma's first question is not about the date or the venue. It is, "What's their sign?" And then, quite often, grandma goes quiet for a moment and says she will ask the family's usual master.

For our grandparents' generation, matching the births of a bride and groom was a natural step before a wedding, as natural as meeting the extended family. The tradition has a name: he hun (合婚), literally "matching the marriage". This article explains what is actually compared, what the terms you may hear mean, and how to approach the result.

If you are not yet sure what the four pillars, heavenly stems or day master are, start with our [introduction to Ba Zi](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tradisi-wedding/mengenal-ba-zi-empat-pilar-kelahiran).

## Where the tradition comes from

In the classical sequence of Chinese wedding customs known as the six rites, one of the first steps was to ask for the bride's name and date of birth. The eight characters of both partners were then matched by a go-between or a diviner. If the result was good, the news went to the bride's family with betrothal gifts, and the sequence moved on to the presentation of gifts. The sangjit we know today descends from that gift-giving step.

Today almost no couple calls off a wedding because of a chart. But the habit of asking about compatibility lives on, and it often comes up at the same moment it always did: after the lamaran, before the sangjit.

## The three things compared

The most common he hun reading looks at three points in the two charts. Each stands for a different side of life.

**The zodiac sign, or the branch of the year pillar.** This is the side of family and social circle. Tradition reads it as a sign of whether the two extended families will get on easily.

**The spouse palace, or the branch of the day pillar.** This is the side of the household and everyday life together. For couples, many practitioners treat this point as the most important, more so than the sign.

**The day master, or the stem of the day pillar.** This is each person's nature. The question is whether the two natures support each other, restrain each other, or pair with each other.

## Combinations, harmonies and clashes between signs

Relations between earthly branches, whether in the signs or the spouse palaces, follow the same rules. Four come up most often.

**The Six Combinations (六合, liu he).** Six pairs of branches considered to complete each other: Rat with Ox, Tiger with Pig, Rabbit with Dog, Dragon with Rooster, Snake with Monkey, and Horse with Goat.

**The Three Harmonies (三合, san he).** Four groups of three signs considered to share a direction: Monkey, Rat and Dragon; Pig, Rabbit and Goat; Tiger, Horse and Dog; and Snake, Rooster and Ox. Two signs from the same group are read as working well together.

**Clashes (冲, chong).** Six pairs of signs that sit opposite each other in the circle of twelve: Rat with Horse, Ox with Goat, Tiger with Monkey, Rabbit with Rooster, Dragon with Dog, and Snake with Pig.

**Harms (害, hai).** Six pairs read as small frictions: Rat with Goat, Ox with Horse, Tiger with Snake, Rabbit with Dragon, Monkey with Pig, and Rooster with Dog.

Clash is the most feared word and the most misunderstood. In the tradition, a clash is read as two energies moving in opposite directions. The result can be friction, and it can also be a push to change. Many Ba Zi practitioners themselves stress that a single clash in a single branch is not enough to conclude anything about a relationship, because seven other characters are read alongside it.

## Day masters that pair

Heavenly stems have partners too. There are five classic pairs: 甲 with 己, 乙 with 庚, 丙 with 辛, 丁 with 壬, and 戊 with 癸. If the two partners' day masters form one of these pairs, tradition reads it as one of the classic signs of a destined match.

Interestingly, each of these pairs also means that one element controls the other. 丙 is fire and 辛 is metal, and fire melts metal. Practitioners read such a bond as a strong pull, with one side tending to lead. Not bad news, but something worth knowing from the start.

Beyond the classic pairs, day masters are also read through the cycle of the five elements. If your day master's element feeds your partner's, such as water nourishing wood, tradition reads you as the one who tends to support. If it controls, such as wood breaking through earth, you are the one who tends to lead.

## A worked reading: Raka and Nadia

To make this concrete, here is the reading for a fictional couple, the same one we use as the example in our [Ba Zi Couple Calculator](https://7magicwedding.com/wedding-tools/kalkulator-bazi-pasangan). Raka was born on 5 May 1995 at 10:00 WIB. Nadia was born on 14 April 1996 at 07:30 WITA.

| | Year | Month | Day | Hour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Raka | 乙亥 | 庚辰 | 丙申 | 癸巳 |
| Nadia | 丙子 | 壬辰 | 辛巳 | 壬辰 |

**Signs.** Raka is a Pig, Nadia a Rat. The two form neither a combination nor a clash, so on the family side tradition has nothing in particular to note.

**Spouse palaces.** Raka's day branch is 申 (Monkey), Nadia's is 巳 (Snake). Snake and Monkey are one of the Six Combinations. For the household side, this is a warm reading.

**Day masters.** Raka's day master is 丙, Yang Fire. Nadia's is 辛, Yin Metal. 丙 and 辛 are one of the five classic pairs, with Raka's fire controlling Nadia's metal.

**Element balance.** Nadia's chart has no wood at all and is heaviest in water. Raka's chart has one wood. Some practitioners read this as two charts that fill each other's gaps.

The overall reading: plenty of harmony. Then they checked two candidate reception dates. Saturday 26 September 2026 is a 癸卯 (Rabbit) day, which shares a group with Raka's Pig sign, so it is safe. Saturday 10 October 2026 is a 丁巳 (Snake) day. The Snake clashes with Raka's Pig sign, even though at the same time it combines with his spouse palace. In choosing a date, a clash with either partner's sign is usually enough for the family to look for another day, so 10 October was struck out.

We chose this example on purpose because it shows something important: a single day can carry a combination and a clash at once. That is why a Ba Zi reading always looks at several points, never at just one.

## What a simple comparison does not see

The three-point comparison above is the first layer. A practitioner reading for a family usually goes further: the ten-year luck pillars (大运, da yun) of both partners, the element each chart needs, the hidden stems inside each branch, and the years the couple will pass through together. So two practitioners may stress different things for the same couple, and different schools of Ba Zi can read the same thing differently.

Our calculator works out only that first layer. Its result is right for starting a conversation, not for replacing the view of the family's elders.

## If the result shows a clash

Almost every Ba Zi practitioner will tell you the same thing: a clash does not forbid a marriage. It is something to notice and to talk about. A few approaches families commonly take:

1. **Choose the date with more care.** If the signs or spouse palaces clash, many families make sure the sangjit and wedding dates at least add no new clash. This is also why our calculator includes a date check.
2. **Ask someone the family trusts.** If your parents are worried, invite them to take both charts to the family's master or adviser. Parents usually find an explanation from a source they trust more reassuring than one from the internet, this article included.
3. **Talk about it early.** Worries about a chart that go unspoken tend to resurface at the wrong moment, for instance during a small disagreement over the preparations. Better to discuss it openly after the lamaran.

## Frequently asked questions

### Our signs clash. Does that mean we are not compatible?

Not necessarily. The sign is only one of eight characters, and many practitioners consider the spouse palace more decisive for a marriage. Couples with clashing signs who have lived in harmony for decades are not a rare story.

### Which matters more, the sign or the spouse palace?

For the relationship between husband and wife, most practitioners give more weight to the spouse palace and the day master. The sign is more often linked to relations between the two extended families.

### Do we need to know our hours of birth to match as a couple?

No. The sign, the spouse palace and the day master can all be worked out without the hour. The hour makes the chart more complete, and only becomes important if you were born around 23:00 or on a day when the Sun enters a new term.

### Is he hun only for Chinese couples?

No. Anyone's Ba Zi can be worked out, since all it needs is a date and time of birth. In mixed couples, he hun is usually done at the request of the Chinese side of the family, and it often becomes a way for the other family to get to know the tradition.
