At the akad at The St. Regis Jakarta on Monday, 16 June 2025, Alyssa Daguise was given in marriage by her younger brother, Andhika, who acted as her wali, the guardian who formally marries off the bride. She wore a kebaya by Tex Saverio and a Sundanese siger, the traditional crown, on her head. That evening, around one to two hundred guests dressed in black sat down to dinner with the couple.

Three days later the mood turned around. The ngunduh mantu, the reception hosted by the groom's family, at JCC Assembly Hall had a Javanese theme, guests were welcomed by reog Ponorogo, and invitations were prepared for about 3,000 people.

The wedding of Al Ghazali, son of Ahmad Dhani and Maia Estianty, to Alyssa Daguise is worth studying for one reason: they split the events clearly. The bride's Sundanese tradition for the akad, the Javanese tradition for the groom's side's event. Few guests for the akad, a big crowd for the ngunduh mantu. This pattern works for a wedding of any size.

## How the celebrations unfolded

- **Friday, 13 June 2025.** Alyssa's pengajian, a Quran recital and prayer gathering, and siraman in the Sundanese tradition at Taman Kajoe, Cilandak.
- **Saturday, 14 June 2025.** Al's siraman and sungkeman in Pondok Indah.
- **Monday, 16 June 2025.** The akad in the Sundanese tradition at The St. Regis Jakarta, followed by an intimate dinner that evening.
- **Thursday, 19 June 2025.** The ngunduh mantu, themed "Javanese Royal Wedding", at JCC Assembly Hall.

## Two siraman on two days

The siraman, the ritual bathing, was held separately for each of them, on two consecutive days. Alyssa had her pengajian and siraman in the Sundanese tradition, while Al had his siraman and sungkeman, the ritual of asking his parents' blessing, the next day.

Separating the siraman like this gives each family its own time. Parents don't have to move between venues on the same day, and each side can follow its own customs. If your families keep different traditions, a back-to-back schedule like this also eases the pressure on makeup and decor vendors.

## A Sundanese akad

The akad followed the Sundanese tradition, the tradition of Alyssa's mother, who comes from Garut. The choice follows a custom that's still common in Indonesia: the akad follows the bride's family's tradition.

Three names were behind the attire. Alyssa's kebaya was designed by **Tex Saverio**, and it was the first kebaya he had ever made. Al wore a beskap from the **Agus Lim x Studio BOH** collaboration. The siger on Alyssa's head was made by **Rinaldy A. Yunardi**.

This combination is worth copying. The siger is one of the most recognisable markers of the Sundanese tradition, so as long as it's there, the kebaya can be designed more freely. If your budget is limited, consider renting a beautiful siger and ordering a kebaya to your taste, or the other way round.

As for the wali, a brother acting as guardian is common at an akad. If the father can't serve as wali, make sure who will take his place has been discussed with the family and the KUA, the religious affairs office, from the start.

## A 16.6-gram mahar

The mahar was **16.6 grams** of gold bullion and **€2,025** in cash. The 2,025 clearly points to the year, and several outlets read the 16.6 grams as the date of the akad, 16 June.

A mahar that holds the date is a popular choice these days, and it's easy to do. The gold weight can be scaled to what you can afford, as long as the numbers still read as a date. The currency is up to you as well. Rupiah is just as valid, and just as meaningful.

## An intimate dinner after the akad

On the evening after the akad, the couple hosted a dinner for about 100 to 200 guests. Guests were asked to wear black, and the couple sat together at a **sweetheart table**, a small table just for the two of them.

A format like this suits couples who want the akad to feel close. With a limited guest count, the meal can be served seated rather than as a buffet, and the couple has time to greet every table. A single-colour dress code also makes the photos look tidy without heavy decoration.

## A Javanese-flavoured ngunduh mantu

The event at JCC Assembly Hall was themed "Javanese Royal Wedding". Guests were welcomed with a reog Ponorogo performance, and invitations were prepared for around 3,000 people.

On stage, Ari Lasso sang with Dul on piano, and Dewa 19 performed. As for the food, one of the menus came from Wong Solo, and guests could also enjoy coffee from Kopi Dewa19.

The lesson here is how roles are shared between families. The ngunduh mantu is the groom's side's event, so it's natural for its tradition, guests and mood to follow the groom's family. That way both families feel they have an event of their own, without forcing two traditions into one day.

For a big event, food from restaurants your guests already know is worth considering too. Guests know what it tastes like, and you don't have to guess whether a dish will work for thousands of people.

## What you can borrow

- **Split traditions by event.** The bride's family's tradition for the akad, the groom's family's for the ngunduh mantu.
- **Vary the guest count by event.** The akad and dinner for those closest to you, the big event for everyone you know.
- **Schedule the siraman on different days.** Each family gets its own time, and vendors don't have to move venues in one day.
- **Centre the traditional element on one key piece.** The right siger is enough to mark a Sundanese wedding.
- **Use a dress code for guests.** One colour for an intimate event makes it feel cohesive.
- **Choose food your guests already know for a big event.** A menu from a familiar restaurant is the safer bet for thousands of people.
