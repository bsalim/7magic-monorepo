About 70% of Singaporeans want a destination wedding or have already had one, according to a 2024 Skyscanner and OnePoll survey quoted by Moneysmart, and Bali is one of the closest options. The ceremony is the easy part to picture. The paperwork is less obvious, because Indonesia asks a foreign partner for proof that they are free to marry, and Singapore's Registry of Marriages (ROM) does not issue the usual document for that.

This guide covers the Indonesian civil step, what Singapore can give you instead of a certificate of no impediment, and whether you need to register the marriage in Singapore afterwards. There is also a short section for your guests on ang bao. We checked the rules against official Singapore and Indonesian sources in early November 2026. This is general information, not legal advice, so confirm details with the offices handling your papers.

## How a Bali wedding becomes legal

Indonesia has no civil-only wedding. Under Article 2 of the Marriage Law (Law No. 1 of 1974), a marriage is valid when it is performed under the law of each partner's religion and belief, and every marriage must be recorded. Muslim couples marry through the KUA, the Office of Religious Affairs. Everyone else holds the religious ceremony first and then registers it with the civil registry, Dukcapil.

Notice goes to the registrar where you will marry at least 10 working days before the wedding (Government Regulation 9/1975, Article 3). Our guide to [getting legally married in Bali](https://7magicwedding.com/en/legally-married-in-bali) explains the two routes couples choose between, a legal wedding in Bali or a legal marriage at home with a symbolic ceremony in Bali, and what Indonesian law asks of couples of different religions. It was written for Australians, but the Indonesian side applies to you too.

## What the Indonesian registry asks of the foreign partner

For a non-Muslim wedding, Presidential Regulation 96/2018 (Article 37) lists what the civil registry needs from a foreign partner:

- A letter from the religious leader confirming the marriage took place
- Colour photos of the couple
- A passport
- A residence letter, for holders of a limited stay permit (KITAS)
- Permission to marry from your own country or its embassy

For a Muslim wedding at the KUA, where one partner is Indonesian, Religious Affairs Regulation 30/2024 (Article 4(3)) asks the foreign partner for a certificate of no impediment from their embassy or, for countries in the Apostille Convention, a marital status document from their home authority with a copy of the apostille. It also asks for copies of the birth certificate and passport, a divorce or death certificate if either of you was married before, and your parents' details. Documents in a foreign language, except Malay, must be translated into Indonesian by an official translator. If you are both Singaporean, ask the KUA where you will marry which documents it wants.

## What Singapore issues instead of a CNI

ROM states that it does not issue a letter of no impediment or a bachelor or single status document. What it suggests instead is a search of its marriage records, which shows whether you have a marriage on record in Singapore, and it adds that you should check whether the authority abroad accepts the result. Viewing the search online is free for citizens and PRs (up to two searches in 12 months), and a digital copy of the result costs S$35. The Ministry of Social and Family Development also confirms it does not issue proof of marital status, and says citizens can have the search result legalised if needed.

Singapore and Indonesia are both in the Apostille Convention, so a Singapore document for Indonesia is apostilled by the Singapore Academy of Law (SAL) rather than legalised by an embassy. The Singapore Embassy in Jakarta sees visitors by appointment only, booked by email. Its services page says it generally does not do notarial work on documents meant for use outside Singapore, unless the local authorities require it.

What a Bali registry will accept from a Singaporean can differ from one office to the next. This is a step where couples often get stuck, and it is one our Bali team handles: preparing the no-impediment documents with you and taking care of the civil registration with Dukcapil.

## If you are Muslim

A Muslim wedding in Bali goes through the KUA under the rules above. Back home, the Registry of Muslim Marriages (ROMM) says it is not empowered to register a Muslim marriage solemnised overseas. The Indonesian marriage certificate is what you use in Singapore, subject to each agency's own requirements.

## After the wedding: registering in Singapore

Registering an overseas marriage with ROM is voluntary. ROM says it is not a legal requirement under the Women's Charter, that you don't need it to deal with Singapore government agencies, and that a marriage certificate issued by the competent authority of another country is accepted as evidence of the marriage unless there is reason to doubt it.

You have three options, and you can pick more than one.

| Option | Required? | Cost | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|
| Use the Indonesian certificate as it is | No action needed | None | Your Indonesian marriage certificate as proof |
| Update your marital status in Singpass | Optional | Free | Easier dealings with digital services such as HDB and CPF; not a certificate |
| Register the overseas marriage at ROM | Optional | S$75, or S$380 if both of you are foreigners | A Singapore marriage certificate showing the overseas marriage details |

To register at ROM, apply online through the Our Marriage Journey portal; walk-ins are not accepted. ROM may take up to two weeks to review the documents, and the S$75 fee is paid by NETS at the ROM office on the day of your appointment. You bring the original Indonesian certificate, both identity documents, and any divorce or death certificate from a previous marriage. If your foreign partner holds or has held a Singapore work permit, MOM approval is required before the wedding, including a wedding in Bali. ROM's checklist asks for a copy of the certificate endorsed by Indonesia's foreign ministry or apostilled, then by the Singapore Embassy, or else by the Indonesian Embassy in Singapore, plus an endorsed English translation if the certificate is not in English. The Singapore Embassy in Jakarta says documents with an apostille can be presented in Singapore without further legalisation. Indonesian apostilles come from the Ministry of Law's legal administration directorate (AHU). Confirm with ROM which version it wants before you apply.

## Guests: ang bao for a Bali wedding

Searches for a "Bali wedding ang bao rate" are common, and there is no single answer. Moneysmart's 2025 guide to destination wedding ang bao, by Audrey Ng, gives no fixed figure. It says guests often accept that the ang bao may be lower than for a banquet at home, because they are already paying for flights and hotels, and that some couples tell guests their presence is enough. Closeness to the couple still matters, and the guide suggests a gift, or help with transport and local arrangements, if cash is tight.

If you are the couple, the kindest thing is to say what you'd like on the invitation or in the group chat. Guests who have booked flights to Bali appreciate not having to guess.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a Singaporean legally marry in Bali?

Yes. The wedding follows Indonesian law, which means a religious ceremony followed by registration with Dukcapil, or a Muslim wedding at the KUA, plus documents showing you are free to marry.

### Does Singapore recognise a marriage registered in Bali?

ROM says a certificate issued by the competent authority of another country is accepted as evidence of the marriage, unless there is reason to doubt its validity. Registering it at ROM is optional.

### Can we have a symbolic ceremony in Bali and marry legally at ROM?

Yes. If both of you are Singapore citizens or PRs, the 31-day stay rule does not apply, and you apply at least 21 days before the ROM date.
