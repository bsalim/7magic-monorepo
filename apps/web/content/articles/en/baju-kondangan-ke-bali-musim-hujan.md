The invitation lands in the family group chat with one line under the date: "Dress code: tropical garden formal." Then the same question arrives from three different people. Your aunt wants to know whether her lace kebaya will do. Your cousin asks whether heels are safe at a clifftop venue. Your dad only wants to know if he really has to pack a suit for Bali in December.

This guide is for anyone going to a Bali wedding as a guest in the wet season, roughly November to March. It sticks to the practical side: the weather to plan for, how to read the venue type from the invitation, options for men, and what to wear if the ceremony is held at or near a Balinese temple.

## What Bali's weather is like from November to March

According to BMKG, Indonesia's weather agency, the 2026/2027 wet season starts in parts of Bali in November 2026 and across most of the island in December 2026. The peak is expected in January 2027 for central Bali and February 2027 for most other areas. BMKG's Bali climatology station expects this wet season to run until April 2027.

Long-term averages tell the same story. Climate normals for the Ngurah Rai station near the airport record about 327 mm of rain in December and 351 mm in January, against under 40 mm in each of July and August. The average temperature sits around 28°C through the wet season. Ubud is wetter still: Copernicus climate model data for 1991 to 2020 puts Ubud's annual rainfall at around 2,400 mm, well above the coast near the airport.

So plan your outfit on the assumption that it will rain at least once while you're in Bali, and that the air will feel humid even when the sky is clear.

## Read the venue from the invitation

Dress codes on Bali wedding invitations are often loose. The more useful clue is the venue's name. Look it up on a map, check photos of the spot, and ask the couple where the ceremony and the reception are, because they're often in different places.

### Clifftop venues

Clifftop venues in Uluwatu and Jimbaran usually mean lawns, stone stairs and a sea breeze. Stilettos sink into wet grass, so go for block heels, wedges or smart flats. The wind also makes a light, very full skirt hard to manage, so a midi cut in a fabric with a bit of weight is more comfortable.

### Beach venues

On the beach, wet sand and closed shoes never get along. Many guests choose flat sandals, or bring a second pair for the part of the ceremony on the sand. Skip floor-sweeping hems, which will be wet and sandy before the reception even starts.

### Jungle and rice-field venues

Venues in and around Ubud often sit in a valley or on the edge of rice fields. Stone paths get slippery after rain and the ground stays damp. Rubber soles are safer than slick leather ones. Pack insect repellent too, especially for an afternoon event.

### Hotel ballrooms

If the reception is in a hotel ballroom in Nusa Dua or Kuta, the usual wedding-guest rules for a city wedding apply almost unchanged. The one thing to remember is the temperature gap. It's humid outside and cold indoors under the air conditioning, so a light wrap or jacket comes in handy.

## Bali wedding guest dress: fabrics and cuts that handle humidity

When the air is humid, the fabric matters more for comfort than the style. A few rules of thumb we often give guests:

- Choose breathable fabrics like linen, cotton or a blend of the two. Heavy synthetics quickly feel hot and sticky.
- Very pale solid colours and shiny satin show sweat marks and raindrops easily. A print or a small texture is more forgiving.
- Midi length, or just above the ankle, is the safest for grass, sand and stairs.
- Pack a spare outfit if there's more than one event, say a welcome dinner and the reception, because clothes soaked in rain rarely dry within a day.

## Bali wedding outfits for men

A full suit is rarely needed at a Bali wedding, unless the invitation clearly says "black tie" or "formal". For a clifftop, beach or garden event, the usual choice is a lightweight batik or linen shirt, light-coloured tailored trousers or chinos, and loafers or leather shoes with some grip. If the dress code says "semi formal", a linen jacket without a tie is usually enough.

If you're invited to a traditional Balinese ceremony, there's a better option. Payas alit for men, the simple traditional dress worn to the temple, is a white shirt or safari shirt with a kamen (a wrapped cloth) and an udeng (headcloth). Ask the couple's family whether guests are expected to wear it. If so, a kamen and udeng are easy to buy in Bali, so there's no need to bring them from home.

## If the ceremony is at or near a temple

Some Bali weddings, especially traditional Balinese ones, include a ceremony at a family or village temple. Other couples add a blessing or photos near a temple. Here the dress rules are different from an ordinary wedding.

Bali Governor's Circular No. 7 of 2025 asks foreign tourists to dress modestly and appropriately, especially when visiting sacred places, and not to enter the main and middle sections of a temple except to pray. It's aimed at foreign visitors, but Indonesian guests are expected to follow the same spirit.

What's asked of you at a temple is a kamen, a cloth wrapped from the waist to cover the legs, plus a selendang, a sash tied at the waist to hold the kamen in place. At Pura Ulun Danu Beratan, for example, a kamen is required for men and women, while the sash is optional. For women, payas alit is a kebaya, a kamen and a selendang. Some temples lend or include sarongs and sashes at the entrance, but don't count on that when you're a wedding guest.

A few more things to keep in mind:

- Women who are menstruating don't enter the temple grounds. It's a common rule at Balinese temples, so let the couple's family know politely if you need to wait outside.
- Follow the lead of the pemangku (temple priest) or the ceremony organisers, because rules differ between temples and villages.
- Don't photograph parts of the ritual that aren't meant to be photographed, and don't block anyone who is praying.

## Going to a traditional Balinese wedding

If the couple is Balinese and the wedding follows custom at the family home or in the village, the safest move is to ask. Some families hope guests will come in a kebaya, kamen and selendang, while others don't mind either way. What to avoid is copying the wedding couple's outfit. Payas agung, the combination of red, gold and white with Balinese songket, is worn only by the bride and groom.

One more thing that often surprises guests from outside Bali: a traditional Balinese wedding date is chosen by dewasa ayu, the Balinese system of auspicious days, not by the weekend. The wedding may well fall on a Wednesday, so you'll need to take leave. ## If it rains during the wedding

Wet-season weddings usually have a covered backup plan, but guests still need to be ready. What's worth having in your bag:

- A compact umbrella that fits a small bag, because the venue's umbrellas often don't stretch to every guest.
- Spare sandals in a plastic bag, especially if you're wearing fabric shoes.
- A small waterproof pouch for your phone and wallet.
- Blotting papers and a little loose powder.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do guests have to wear traditional Balinese dress?

No, unless the couple asks you to or you'll be entering a temple. For a wedding at a hotel, villa or beach club, follow the dress code on the invitation.

### Can I wear sandals to a Bali wedding?

At beach and garden venues, smart sandals are generally fine, and often more practical. For a hotel ballroom with a formal dress code, choose closed shoes.

### What's the safest Bali wedding guest outfit for men?

A lightweight batik or linen shirt with tailored trousers works at almost any venue. For a traditional ceremony, ask whether guests are expected to wear a white shirt, kamen and udeng.
