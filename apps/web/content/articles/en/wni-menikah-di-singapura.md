The wedding day at ROM goes by quickly. What takes longer is everything after it. A few weeks later your Indonesian mother-in-law asks in the family group chat, "Has it been registered back home yet? What about the family card?" It's a fair question, because a marriage that is valid in Singapore is not automatically on record in Indonesia.

This guide walks through the whole route: the Registry of Marriages (ROM) requirements, the apostille for the certificate, reporting to the Indonesian Embassy in Singapore, and registering the marriage with Indonesia's civil registry (Dukcapil) afterwards, plus the religious affairs office (KUA) for Muslim couples. We checked every rule below against official sources in early November 2026. This is general information, not legal advice, and procedures change, so confirm with the office handling your papers.

## Valid in Singapore, then recorded in Indonesia

Article 56 of Indonesia's Marriage Law (Law No. 1 of 1974) says a marriage of an Indonesian citizen abroad is valid if it follows the law of the country where it takes place and, for the Indonesian partner, does not breach the Marriage Law. Indonesia's Population Administration Law (Law No. 23 of 2006, Article 37) then requires the marriage to be registered with the local authority and reported to the Indonesian mission.

In practice the order is:

1. Marry and register at ROM in Singapore.
2. Get an apostille for the marriage certificate from the Singapore Academy of Law (SAL).
3. Report the marriage to the Indonesian Embassy in Singapore.
4. Report it to the civil registry (Dukcapil) where the Indonesian partner lives after returning; Muslim couples also register the proof of marriage at the KUA.

## ROM requirements

ROM handles civil marriages. Muslim couples marry through the Registry of Muslim Marriages (ROMM), which has its own rules and is not covered here. For ROM, the official guide (October 2026 version) sets out the main requirements.

**The 31-day stay.** If either of you is not a Singapore citizen or permanent resident (PR), at least one of you must have been physically in Singapore for 31 continuous days, not counting the day of arrival, before the application is submitted. The 31 days don't have to come right before you apply. A single long visit in the past counts.

**Apply at least 21 days ahead.** The application is made online through the Our Marriage Journey portal at least 21 days before the wedding date, and no earlier than six months before it.

**Age and status.** At 21 or over you need no one's consent. Anyone aged 18 to under 21 who has not been married before needs a parent's or guardian's consent and must attend a marriage preparation programme. Under 18, the couple must apply for a Special Marriage Licence. Neither of you can be currently married.

**Documents.** Your Indonesian partner uses their passport. You also need two witnesses over 21, plus a divorce certificate or a late spouse's death certificate where relevant. If neither of you is a Singaporean and neither lives or works in Singapore, ROM asks for a certificate of no impediment from each partner's embassy or home authority. Documents in other languages must be translated into English and authenticated. Ask ROM and the Indonesian Embassy which documents they accept before your partner starts gathering them in Indonesia.

**Declaration at ROM.** If one of you has no Singpass, or foreign documents are involved, the declaration and document check are done in person at ROM. This appointment is usually 1 to 14 days before the wedding.

**Work permit holders.** This one is easy to miss. A current or former work permit holder, including a Training Work Permit holder, who wants to marry a Singapore citizen or PR must get approval from the Ministry of Manpower (MOM) before submitting the marriage application. MOM takes about four weeks.

| Official ROM fees | Amount |
|---|---|
| Marriage application, at least one partner a citizen or PR | S$42 |
| Marriage application, both partners foreigners | S$380 |
| Changing the solemnisation details after submitting | S$25 |

Venue, an external solemniser and the reception are not included and vary by venue.

## Where ROM is now

ROM's building at Canning Rise is being redeveloped. Since 14 April 2025, ROM and ROMM have been at a temporary site on Level 3 of Esplanade Mall. The first announcement put the works at 2025 to 2028, but according to The Straits Times in February 2026, the Ministry of Social and Family Development (MSF) now says both will return to Canning Rise in 2029. If an older guide sends you to Canning Rise, that address does not apply for now.

You don't have to marry in the ROM building either. The solemnisation can take place at a hotel or another venue, with a licensed solemniser you choose when you apply.

## The digital certificate and the SAL apostille

Once the marriage is registered, the digital marriage certificate arrives by email as a password-protected PDF. To be used in Indonesia, it needs an apostille.

Singapore became a party to the Apostille Convention on 16 September 2021, and Indonesia on 4 June 2022. The Indonesian Embassy in Singapore states that Singapore documents for use in Indonesia only need an apostille from the Singapore Academy of Law, with no further legalisation at the embassy. Civil documents such as a marriage certificate must already carry the SAL apostille before the embassy will record them. SAL's legalisation site says digitally issued documents must be printed first. Check fees and processing times on the SAL site directly, as we found no official figure we could quote.

## Reporting to the Indonesian Embassy

Presidential Regulation No. 96 of 2018 governs this report. For two Indonesian citizens (Article 38), the requirements are the marriage certificate from Singapore and both partners' Indonesian passports. For an Indonesian marrying a foreign national (Article 40), they are the Singapore certificate or proof of registration, both partners' passports, and a letter showing domicile or a certificate of moving abroad.

RRI Batam, citing Indonesia's Foreign Ministry on 31 July 2026, described how it works at the embassy in Singapore:

- Register online for the embassy's public services before you go.
- Bring original passports with copies, identity cards, and the ROM or ROMM marriage certificate with its SAL apostille.
- Fill in the embassy's application form and document checklist.
- The service is free and takes about two working days once the file is complete.

The embassy offers no photocopying and does not sell duty stamps, so bring what you need. Two sources give different counter hours: the embassy's legalisation page says 9.00am to 12.00pm, while RRI says 9.30am to 12.00pm. Go early, or ask at singapura.kbri@kemlu.go.id. The address is 7 Chatsworth Road.

## Registering after returning: Dukcapil, plus the KUA for Muslim couples

Two Indonesian rules give different deadlines here, and you should know both.

The Population Administration Law (Law 23/2006, Article 37(4)) requires a marriage abroad to be reported to the civil registry where the Indonesian partner lives **within 30 days of returning to Indonesia**. This applies to every Indonesian citizen, Muslim couples included, and the 2013 amendment (Law 24/2013) left it unchanged. For two Indonesian citizens, Article 39 of Presidential Regulation 96/2018 sets the requirements as the embassy's proof of reporting and the marriage certificate. The Jakarta registry, for example, also asks for a translation of the foreign marriage certificate, birth certificates, ID and family cards, passports, and 4x6 photos of the couple side by side.

The Marriage Law, Article 56(2), allows **one year** after the couple return to Indonesia. For Muslim couples, Minister of Religious Affairs Regulation No. 30 of 2024 (Article 33) also uses a one-year limit, with registration at the KUA where they live. They bring the proof of marriage from abroad and the embassy's proof of reporting. After one year, they sign a statement on the documents' accuracy and the reason for the delay. Registering at the KUA does not replace the 30-day report to Dukcapil. Muslim couples do both.

| Step | Where | Deadline | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Report the marriage | Indonesian Embassy in Singapore | After registering at ROM | Presidential Regulation 96/2018, Articles 38 and 40 |
| Population registration | Civil registry where you live | 30 days after returning | Law 23/2006, Article 37(4) |
| Register the proof of marriage | KUA where you live, for Muslim couples | 1 year after returning | Religious Affairs Regulation 30/2024, Article 33 |

Our advice is simple: treat 30 days as your deadline. Late reporting of a civil event can bring an administrative fine of up to Rp1,000,000 (Law 23/2006, Article 90), with the amount set regionally. Call the local registry or KUA before going in, as the document list can differ slightly between cities.

## If you are the Singaporean partner

Marrying a Singaporean does not automatically give your partner the right to live in Singapore. The official marriage portal encourages couples to do ICA's Pre-Marriage Long-Term Visit Pass Assessment (PMLA) before marrying. It's free and takes about a month. With a positive outcome, the Long-Term Visit Pass application after the wedding takes about six weeks instead of six months.

Couples of different nationalities should also talk about a marriage agreement before the wedding. For the Indonesian partner, a separation-of-property agreement made by notarial deed in Indonesia decides whether they can keep holding land rights like any other Indonesian. We cover it in our article on [prenups when marrying an Indonesian](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tips-hubungan/perjanjian-pranikah-perkawinan-campuran-wna).

One last note on interfaith marriages. The Supreme Court's circular SEMA 2/2023 tells Indonesian courts not to grant petitions to register interfaith marriages. It does not deal specifically with marriages abroad, but registering one in Indonesia can still be contested, and Religious Affairs Regulation 30/2024 (Article 33(5)) says proof of a marriage abroad that was not conducted in line with the law cannot be registered at the KUA. Take legal advice before you apply.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to marry in Singapore?

ROM's official application fee is S$42 if one of you is a Singapore citizen or PR, and S$380 if both of you are foreigners. On top of that come the venue, a solemniser if you marry outside the ROM building, the apostille and travel. Reporting to the Indonesian Embassy is free.

### Do we both need to stay 31 days in Singapore?

No. One of you is enough, and only when one partner is not a Singapore citizen or PR. The day of arrival does not count.

### Is a ROM marriage valid in Indonesia?

Yes, as long as it follows Singapore law and does not breach the Marriage Law for the Indonesian partner. It only enters Indonesia's population records once it has been reported to the embassy and recorded by Dukcapil (Muslim couples also register it at the KUA).

### Does the ROM certificate need translating?

The certificate is in English. The Jakarta civil registry lists a translation of the foreign marriage certificate among its requirements. Ask the registry handling your file whether it must be done by a sworn translator.
