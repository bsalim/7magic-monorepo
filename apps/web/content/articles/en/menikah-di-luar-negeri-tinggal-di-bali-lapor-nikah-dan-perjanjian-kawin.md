The story is often the same. You married in Melbourne, Singapore or Amsterdam. A few years later you moved to Bali, the Indonesian spouse found the right plot or villa, and then the notary or the bank asked: is there a separation-of-property agreement, and has your marriage been reported in Indonesia?

Those two questions are connected. This article explains the order, from reporting the marriage to a registered marital agreement, so your purchase does not stall halfway.

## Why the order matters

Without a separation-of-property agreement, property acquired during the marriage is joint property (Article 35 of the Marriage Law). For a mixed couple, that rule collides with the Basic Agrarian Law: a foreigner who acquires freehold (Hak Milik) through the mixing of marital property must give it up within one year (Article 21(3)). That is why Government Regulation No. 18 of 2021, Article 70, only lets an Indonesian married to a foreigner hold freehold or building rights (HGB) if the land is not joint property, proven by a separation-of-property agreement made as a notarial deed. We go into more detail in [prenuptial agreements for mixed marriages](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tips-hubungan/perjanjian-pranikah-perkawinan-campuran-wna).

The catch is that a marital agreement is registered as a note on the record of your marriage in Indonesia. If your marriage has never been reported here, there is no record to add the note to. So the order is always the same: report the marriage, make the agreement, register the agreement, then buy.

## Step 1: Report your marriage in Indonesia

A marriage of an Indonesian citizen abroad is valid if it was carried out under the law of the country where it took place (Article 56(1) of the Marriage Law). But valid is not enough for Indonesian paperwork. The marriage also has to be reported.

On the deadline, two laws disagree. Marriage Law Article 56(2) gives one year from the couple's return to Indonesia. Population Administration Law Article 37 asks for the report within 30 days of return, and late reporting can carry an administrative fine under regional regulations. If you have lived in Bali for a while without reporting, there is no need to panic, but do not put it off any longer.

The report is made at the Disdukcapil (civil registry) where the Indonesian spouse lives. The documents usually requested:

- The marriage certificate from the country where you married.
- An Indonesian translation of that certificate by a sworn translator.
- Both spouses' passports, plus the Indonesian spouse's electronic ID card (KTP) and Family Card (KK).
- Proof of reporting to the Indonesian embassy or consulate in the country where you married, if you reported at the time.

Since 4 June 2022 Indonesia has been a party to the Apostille Convention. If the country where you married is also a party, its documents only need an apostille from that country's authority, without embassy legalisation. Each Disdukcapil's exact list can differ, so check first with the office in your regency or city in Bali.

## Step 2: Sign a marital agreement before a notary in Bali

Since Constitutional Court Decision 69/PUU-XIII/2015, a marital agreement can be made during the marriage. How it works, including the choice of when it takes effect, is explained in our [guide to postnuptial agreements](https://7magicwedding.com/artikel/tips-hubungan/perjanjian-kawin-setelah-menikah).

Three points specific to mixed couples in Bali:

- **The notary must be based in Bali** if the deed is signed in Bali. A notary's jurisdiction is the province where they are based (Articles 17 and 18 of the Notary Law). The deed is still valid throughout Indonesia.
- **The foreign spouse must understand the content.** Deeds involving a foreign party are usually made in Indonesian and in a language the foreign party understands, and the notary can be assisted by a sworn translator.
- **Choose the effective date deliberately.** If you have already bought something during the marriage, discuss with the notary whether the agreement takes effect from the wedding date or from the signing, bearing in mind the rights of any existing bank or creditor.

## Step 3: Register the agreement

Once signed, the deed binds the two of you. To bind third parties, including the land office and banks, it is registered with the agency that records your marriage in Indonesia. Because your marriage was reported at the Disdukcapil, registration is usually there too, under Circular Letter of the Director General of Population and Civil Registration No. 472.2/5876/DUKCAPIL. Muslim couples should also confirm with the local KUA (Office of Religious Affairs).

## Step 4: Then buy

With a registered agreement, the Indonesian spouse can buy freehold or HGB in their own name, and the land is not joint property. Bring a copy of the agreement and proof of its registration to the land deed official (PPAT) at the transaction.

One thing this agreement cannot do: make the foreign spouse a co-owner of freehold land. Article 26(2) of the Basic Agrarian Law voids any act intended to transfer freehold to a foreigner, directly or indirectly. If the foreign spouse wants a right over a home of their own, the route is a right of use (Hak Pakai), discussed with the PPAT at the transaction.

## What about a prenup from back home?

If you signed a *prenuptial agreement* in your home country, keep it. It still matters for your property abroad. But for land in Indonesia, what Government Regulation 18/2021 asks for is a separation-of-property agreement made as a notarial deed. Mixed couples usually make an Indonesian version for property here, which refers to the foreign agreement for property abroad.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do we both have to come to Bali?

For signing the deed, yes, both of you appear before the notary. The consultation and drafting can be done remotely.

### How long does the whole process take?

That depends on reporting the marriage, where the timing is set by the Disdukcapil and by how complete your documents are. Once the marriage is recorded, the marital agreement is usually done within two to three weeks, through to registration.

### We married in Bali, not abroad. Does this apply to us?

If your marriage was registered in Bali, step 1 is not needed because your marriage is already recorded in Indonesia. Go straight to step 2.

### Does a late-reporting fine block the marital agreement?

Any late-reporting fine is settled when you report. Once the marriage is recorded, the marital agreement can be made as normal.
