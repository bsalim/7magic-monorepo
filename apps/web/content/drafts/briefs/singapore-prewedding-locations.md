# Research brief: Unique pre-wedding photoshoot locations in Singapore

Compiled 2026-10-07 for an Indonesian article (title uses "Singapura") and its English version.
Every page cited below was read on **2026-10-07** unless a different date is given. Source markers
[S#] refer to the Sources list at the end.

**How to read this brief**

- Ordinary facts carry at least two independent markers, and Wikipedia counts as at most one of them.
- **Permit rules, fees, booking windows, opening hours and time slots** are operator or agency business data.
  They are quoted only from the official page and marked **(official)**. A second source is added where one exists.
  Fees are quoted exactly as printed, with the read date. **Do not round them, convert them or estimate them.**
- "Not confirmed" means no official page answered the question. Write it that way in the article
  (for example "check with the operator"), and never fill the gap with a guess.
- MRT distances are **straight-line** distances that we calculated from the official OneMap (SLA) geocoder on
  2026-10-07 [S35]. They are not walking times. Use them only to choose the "nearest station", not to say
  "X minutes on foot".
- **Key framing problem for the article:** Singapore agencies split shoots into *personal* (no permit)
  and *commercial* (permit). Most of them **do not say which side a pre-wedding shoot with a paid photographer
  falls on**. Gardens by the Bay, National Gallery Singapore and Jewel are the exceptions: they name wedding
  shoots and sell bookings for them. For every other place the honest line is "ask the agency in writing first".
  - Gardens by the Bay settles it for itself: its packages are "strictly for **personal** filming or photography
    sessions such as wedding, graduation, maternity or family shoots" (official) [S13], and its older terms list
    "pre-wedding photoshoot" as personal use [S14]. Jewel keeps weddings as their own category, separate from
    commercial [S19][S20]. No other agency in this brief says either way (re-checked 2026-10-07).

## Overview: general rules

### Permits: who issues what

- **Parks and nature reserves (NParks).** "A permit is required for all filming and commercial photography in
  our parks and nature reserves." No application fee. Apply "at least 1 month before" the shoot, with one
  application per location (official, page updated 2 Sep 2026) [S1].
  - Read literally, the sentence covers *all filming* (video) but only *commercial* photography. Whether NParks
    applies this to a couple's own pre-wedding video is **not confirmed**; do not tell readers a video is exempt.
  - A "small-scale" permit covers fewer than 16 participants, no drones and no bulky equipment such as dolly
    tracks [S1].
  - The small-scale permit **does not cover** nature reserves, nature parks, Fort Canning Park, Singapore
    Botanic Gardens, HortPark, Jurong Lake Gardens, Esplanade Park, Istana Park, Ann Siang Hill Park and
    several other city parks. Those need the "other filming" form or the Botanic Gardens' own form [S1].
  - Conditions under the permit: shoot on weekdays at off-peak times. Not allowed: flash photography, props,
    confetti or flower petals, and any equipment other than a tripod (official) [S1].
  - **CONFLICT**: NParks' Photography page says "at least 2 weeks in advance" [S2]. See the Conflicts section.
  - Shoots in nature reserves and nature parks "are reviewed stringently and approved on a case-by-case basis",
    and applicants whose project is unrelated to conservation are advised to look elsewhere (official) [S1].
- **Singapore Botanic Gardens** (an NParks site with its own rules). "Casual photography sessions (such as
  family portraits and graduations) do not require submission." Submissions are needed at least 14 days before
  the date of use (official) [S3]. Whether a pre-wedding shoot counts as "casual" is **not confirmed**.
- **Streets and walkways (IMDA, the national media authority).** "In general, permits are not required for
  filming on streets and walkways in Singapore, as long as there is no obstruction to vehicular or pedestrian
  traffic." For properties, seek approval from the owner. Tell nearby businesses or neighbours at least
  3 working days ahead as a courtesy (official, IMDA *Guide to Filming*, March 2021 edition) [S9].
  - Common areas of HDB public-housing estates fall under Town Councils (official) [S9].
- **PUB reservoir sites, including Marina Barrage.** "A permit or prior approval is required for doing
  commercial filming and photography in PUB premises" [S54].
  - PUB's form says filming and photography are allowed only from 7am to 7pm. Tracks, cranes and dollies are
    not allowed (official) [S55]. No fee is stated.
- **Sentosa (Sentosa Development Corporation, SDC).** Contact SDC "at least 10 working days in advance for any
  filming/photography requests" (official) [S64]. No fee is stated. Whether personal shoots need approval is
  **not confirmed**.
- **No location in this brief bans shoots outright**, so none was dropped for that reason.
  - **CHIJMES was dropped** because its official shoot policy and fees could not be found.
  - The mosque prayer hall is not a shoot location (see Kampong Glam).

### Drones

- Flying outdoors for business needs a CAAS **Operator Permit and a Class 1 Activity Permit** "(regardless of the
  weight of your UA, the location and height at which you fly your UA)", plus a **UA Pilot Licence**, also
  regardless of weight (UA = unmanned aircraft) (official, page updated 8 May 2026) [S7]. A Class 1 Activity
  Permit is required whenever "the activity is not recreational or educational" [S6]. CAAS does not mention
  weddings; treating a hired photographer's pre-wedding shoot as "business" is our reading, not CAAS wording.
  - CAAS processing fees: Operator Permit S$700 for the first UA type, plus "$200 ... for the purpose of CFMS
    subscription" (CFMS = the flight-management system); Class 1 Activity Permit S$120 per activity or block of
    dates. Operator Permit holders must also attach a CFMS UA tracker, listed at S$265 "(subject to prevailing
    GST)" (official) [S6].
  - Average processing times: 10 working days for an Operator Permit and 5 for an Activity Permit (official,
    page updated 9 May 2026) [S6].
- Any UA heavier than 250 g must be **registered** [S7]. For recreational flying outdoors it must also carry
  **Broadcast Remote ID**; for business flying, CAAS requires Broadcast Remote ID only for a UA over 250 g whose
  operator has a waiver from the FlyItSafe app (official) [S7].
- **No-fly zones** include anywhere within 5 km of an aerodrome, plus danger and prohibited areas. CAAS points
  users to OneMap to check a spot. "National parks, nature reserves and private property may also have local
  restrictions" (official) [S8].
- **Site-level bans (all official):**
  - Gardens by the Bay bans drones in all three gardens [S12].
  - Singapore Botanic Gardens bans flying "model aircraft/drones" [S3]. Its permit terms allow them only with
    CAAS and NParks approval [S4].
  - NParks' small-scale permit excludes drones [S1].
  - Sentosa: "recreational drone flying is strictly NOT allowed" [S64].
  - Jewel requires approval from both CAAS and Jewel, and Changi Airport lies within the 5 km zone [S19][S8].
  - Sultan Mosque: no drone flying or drone photography in the main prayer hall [S40].
- **Practical line for the article:** plan the shoot without a drone. Any drone shot needs CAAS permits and the
  site owner's consent.

### Weather

- Singapore has two monsoon seasons with inter-monsoon periods in between [S10][S11]:
  - Northeast Monsoon: December to early March, per the Meteorological Service Singapore (MSS) [S10].
    **CONFLICT**: Wikipedia starts it in mid-November [S11].
  - Southwest Monsoon: June to September [S10][S11].
- Rainfall is heaviest from November to January, during the wet phase of the Northeast Monsoon [S10].
  - In December and January, Wikipedia notes cloudy conditions with frequent afternoon showers [S11].
- The inter-monsoon periods (late March to May, and October to November) bring afternoon and early-evening
  thunderstorms, sometimes severe [S10]. MSS calls October–November "generally wetter" than the earlier
  inter-monsoon [S10].
- Rain falls more often in the daytime, especially in the afternoon [S10][S11]. **Mornings are usually the safer
  bet, with two exceptions MSS names** [S10]:
  - Southwest Monsoon (June–September): occasional "Sumatra Squalls" with 40–80 km/h gusts strike "between the
    predawn hours and midday".
  - Early Northeast Monsoon (usually December to early January): monsoon surges bring "widespread continuous
    moderate to heavy rain", which is not tied to the afternoon.
  - So write "mornings usually stay drier", not "mornings are safe in every season".
- Humidity is often above 90% before sunrise and falls to about 60% on dry afternoons [S10][S11]. Plan
  hair, make-up and gown changes for this.
- **Rain-proof options in this list:** Gardens by the Bay conservatories, Jewel, National Gallery Singapore
  (indoor venues).

## Locations

Ordered roughly from city centre outward. **(I)** = indoor or rain-proof.

### 1. Fort Canning Park: Sang Nila Utama Garden (plus the Tree Tunnel, with a caveat)

- **Name and area:** Fort Canning Park, a 22 ha hilltop park bounded by Hill Street, Canning Rise, Clemenceau
  Avenue and River Valley Road [S27]. Its Sang Nila Utama Garden is one of nine historical gardens; the
  2019 opening date is from Wikipedia only [S28][S29].
- **MRT:** Fort Canning (DT20), 373 m; Clarke Quay; Dhoby Ghaut [S27][S35]. NParks lists all three as nearby
  [S27].
- **The shot:**
  - The garden recreates a 14th-century Southeast Asian palace garden, with a symmetrical layout, "a series of
    Javanese split gates" and a reflective pool [S28][S29].
  - The split gates are *candi bentar*, a style that traces its roots to the Majapahit Empire [S30b][S30c];
    NParks calls them "Javanese split gates" [S28], and The Straits Times' 2019 park graphic also says
    "Javanese split gates" [S30d]. TheSmartLocal says one is a replica of the Wringin Lawang gate [S30c]
    (single source on Wringin Lawang, do not print it as fact).
  - **Angle for Indonesian readers:** a Majapahit-style gateway in central Singapore. The garden is named after
    Sang Nila Utama, the Palembang prince who founded early Singapore [S29][S30d]; NParks calls him "the first
    ancient king of Singapore" [S28].
- **Tree Tunnel (popular spot, fresh angle = go very early):**
  - A spiral staircase on the edge of the park. Looking up from the bottom, foliage frames a circular opening
    to the sky [S31a][S31b].
  - Both guides tell visitors to arrive early to beat queues [S31a][S31b]. See Conflicts for the exact hour.
  - **NParks sends Tree Tunnel requests to LTA (Land Transport Authority):** "For requests relating to Fort
    Canning Tree Tunnel, please contact the Land Transport Authority" (official) [S27]. NParks does not say who
    owns the staircase, and no LTA page on shoots there was found. Print "ask LTA", not "it is LTA property".
- **Permit (official):**
  - Fort Canning Park is excluded from NParks' small-scale permit, so use the "other filming and commercial
    photography" form [S1].
  - The park page also tells visitors to use a "Park Use Request form for all group activities, photography &
    filming, and solemnization/proposal requests" (CONFLICT, see below) [S27].
  - Fee: none stated ("No application fee is required") [S1]. Lead time: at least 1 month [S1]. No flash,
    no props or confetti, tripod only [S1].
  - Tree Tunnel: LTA's rules and fees **not confirmed**.
- **Hours:** park open 24 hours (official) [S27].

### 2. National Gallery Singapore: colonial courts and City Hall, indoors (I)

- **Name and area:** National Gallery Singapore, in the Civic District facing the Padang. It occupies two
  national monuments, the former Supreme Court and the former City Hall [S24][S23].
- **MRT:** City Hall (NS25/EW13), 274 m; Esplanade (CC3) [S35].
- **The shot:**
  - Colonial columns, corridors and domed spaces in two colonial-era national monuments, joined by bridges and a
    new roof canopy [S24][S23].
  - Bookable areas include the City Hall foyer and corridors, Padang Atrium, Padang Deck, Supreme Court foyers
    (Levels 1 and 3), Supreme Court Terrace, and the Lower and Upper Link Bridges (official) [S23].
  - The former Supreme Court opened in 1939, and the Gallery opened on 24 November 2015 [S24]. The Gallery is
    "the largest visual arts venue" in Singapore [S24]. These details are from Wikipedia only; print them only
    as background.
- **Permit and fees (official, read 2026-10-07)** [S23]:
  - Wedding personal photoshoot: **S$381.50 per group of 5 people (including GST)**. Up to 2 groups per slot.
    Book at least 10 working days ahead.
  - Weekday slots: 7.30–9.30am (before opening), 10.30am–12.30pm and 4–6pm. Weekend slots: 7.30–9.30am only.
  - Black-out dates: eves of public holidays, public holidays and F1 weekend.
  - Gallery Insider members get 15% off.
  - Commercial shoots: 7.30pm–9.30am only, applied for 14 working days ahead, price on enquiry. Security staff
    accompany the group.
  - Restrictions: no artworks in frame (copyright). "Only handheld equipment is allowed." The Link Bridges are
    open only in the pre-opening slot.

### 3. Old Hill Street Police Station: the rainbow windows

- **Name and area:** Old Hill Street Police Station (OHSPS), 140 Hill Street, at the corner of Hill Street and
  River Valley Road [S25][S26].
- **MRT:** Clarke Quay (NE5), 458 m; Fort Canning (DT20), 470 m; City Hall, 524 m [S35]. The building owner
  names City Hall and Clarke Quay as nearest [S25].
- **The shot:**
  - **927 windows painted in rainbow colours** [S25][S26].
  - The first four storeys share one colour intensity. Higher up, the colours "gradually intensify to accentuate
    the cantilevered balconies" [S25][S26].
- **Background:** built in 1934 as a police station and barracks; gazetted a National Monument on 18 December
  1998 [S25][S26]. It now houses MDDI (Ministry of Digital Development and Information) and MCCY (Ministry of
  Culture, Community and Youth) [S25].
- **Permit (official):**
  - MDDI asks people to "write in to request for permission ... to use the premises for filming or photography"
    [S25].
  - It also states: "In general, images of the building can be presented for educational purposes but not for
    commercial use" [S25]. **A photo studio using the shots in its own advertising should ask first.**
  - Shooting from the public pavement: the IMDA street rule applies (no permit if nothing is obstructed) [S9].
  - Fee: **not confirmed**.

### 4. Gardens by the Bay: Flower Dome or Cloud Forest before opening (I) (popular spot, fresh angle)

- **Name and area:** Gardens by the Bay, Bay South Garden, Marina Bay. The Flower Dome and Cloud Forest are two
  cooled conservatories [S18][S16][S15].
- **MRT:** Gardens by the Bay (TE22), Exit 1; Bayfront (CC34/DT16), Exit B, then via the Dragonfly or Meadow
  Bridge [S17][S35][S75]. (The operator's page still prints the retired code CE1.)
- **The shot:**
  - Flower Dome: a cool-dry Mediterranean-climate glasshouse with a changing flower field, kept at 23–25°C
    [S16]. The operator says it "holds the Guinness World Record for the largest glass greenhouse" [S16], and
    Wikipedia agrees [S18].
  - Cloud Forest: a 35 m indoor waterfall and a plant-clad mountain with aerial walkways [S15][S18]. The name
    "Cloud Mountain" is Wikipedia's [S18]; the operator just says "mountain".
  - **Fresh angle:** book the one-hour exclusive pre-opening slot, so the dome is empty of the public.
- **Current exhibitions that affect backgrounds (official):**
  - Cloud Forest currently hosts *Jurassic World: The Experience* with animatronic dinosaurs, so expect them in
    frame [S15][S13].
  - Floral Fantasy has the Disney Garden of Wonder exhibition from 8 Jun 2026 to 14 Mar 2027, and tripods are
    banned there [S13][S12].
  - Flower Dome hosts "Experience Portugal" from 8 Oct to 9 Nov 2026 [S16].
- **Rules (official)** [S12]:
  - Personal filming and photography are welcome. Wedding couples are "strongly encouraged" to shoot on weekdays
    or book a session.
  - **No tripods on weekends and public holidays.**
  - **No drones.**
  - Commercial shoots need prior approval, applied for at least 14 days ahead.
- **Fees (official, "Photography Use" page, read 2026-10-07)** [S13]:
  - **Personal Photoshoot package: S$188 nett.** It includes 4 adult tickets to any 2 of Flower Dome, Cloud
    Forest, Floral Fantasy or Supertree Observatory, a 2-way buggy transfer and 2 hours of free parking.
  - Compulsory S$56 nett surcharge for Cloud Forest sessions (Jurassic World), from 29 May 2025.
  - Third attraction: +S$70, "inclusive of 1 additional hour, 4 admission tickets and buggy services" (the page
    does not say whether this add-on is nett).
  - Friends of the Gardens members (Flower Dome, Cloud Forest & OCBC Skyway memberships) get 20% off.
  - Book by email: weddings@gardensbythebay.com.sg.
  - **Exclusive pre-opening session: S$697.60 Monday–Friday, S$1,308 weekends and public holidays.** These are
    per one-hour session at a single location, stated as "after the 20% discount", and include the buggy
    service. The page frames the 20% as a "special opening rate" promotion, so the price may change; it does not
    say whether GST is included.
  - Pre-opening slots: Flower Dome and Cloud Forest 8–9am, Floral Fantasy 9–10am. No refunds once confirmed.
- **Older official PDF terms (may be outdated, see Conflicts)** [S14]:
  - Apply 14 days ahead; at most 7 persons; a staff member accompanies the group.
  - Make-up and changing are not allowed in public areas or the domes.
  - No props or backdrops; photos for personal use only.
- **Hours (official):**
  - Flower Dome: 9am–9pm, last admission 8.30pm; next maintenance closure 16 Nov 2026 [S16].
  - Cloud Forest: 9am–9pm, last admission 8pm; next closure 26 Oct 2026 [S15].

### 5. Jewel Changi Airport: Canopy Park and the Rain Vortex (I), for couples flying in

- **Name and area:** Jewel Changi Airport, linked to Terminals 1–3 at Changi [S22][S19]. Canopy Park is on
  Level 5 (the top level) [S20][S22].
- **MRT:** Changi Airport (CG2), 343 m [S35]. Jewel is linked to Terminal 1 directly and to Terminals 2 and 3
  by a link bridge [S22][S19].
- **The shot:**
  - The **HSBC Rain Vortex**, a 40 m waterfall billed as the world's tallest indoor waterfall [S21][S22].
  - The terraced Forest Valley around it [S22].
  - Canopy Park's hedge maze, mirror maze and Canopy Bridge [S20][S22].
- **Rules and fees (official)** [S19][S20]:
  - "Application is required for all wedding, commercial and media filming/photography in Jewel." Email
    contact.us@jewelchangiairport.com [S19].
  - Canopy Park wedding package: **S$188 before GST**. It covers the couple plus up to 3 crew at all
    attractions except the Bouncing Net and Walking Net [S20].
  - Add-ons: Jewel Suite (1 hour) and a solemnisation package, prices not stated [S20].
  - Slots: Monday–Thursday only (excluding school holidays and public holidays and their eves),
    10am–12pm or 7–9pm; "Last admission is 30 mins prior to closing time" [S20].
  - The link bridges from Terminals 2 and 3 are Changi Airport premises, not Jewel's: requests go to
    filmphoto@changiairport.com (official) [S19].
  - Enquiries: venuehire@jewelchangiairport.com [S20].
  - Drones: Jewel lies within 5 km of Changi Airport. Flying needs a CAAS permit plus a Jewel filming permit
    [S19][S8].
  - Lead time: **not confirmed**.
- **Hours (official):**
  - Rain Vortex runs 10am–10pm daily [S19].
  - Light & Sound show: Monday–Thursday at 8pm and 9pm; Friday–Sunday and public holidays (and their eves) at
    8pm, 9pm and 10pm [S19].

### 6. Kampong Glam: Sultan Mosque from Bussorah Street, and Haji Lane

- **Name and spelling:**
  - URA and NHB (National Heritage Board) use **Kampong Glam** [S43][S44].
  - Singapore Tourism Board materials now use **Kampong Gelam** [S46].
  - Either is correct; pick one per article. In Indonesian copy, "Kampong Gelam" reads more naturally.
  - The mosque is **Sultan Mosque / Masjid Sultan**, 3 Muscat Street [S39][S41].
- **MRT:** Bugis (EW12/DT14). Haji Lane is about 270 m (DT14 platform) to 367 m (EW12 platform) from Bugis by
  straight line [S35]. The mosque gives Bugis and Jalan Besar (DT22) as about 10 minutes' walk each [S39][S35].
- **The shot:**
  - Two golden onion domes [S41][S42], viewed down Bussorah Street, a short pedestrian street of restaurants and
    shops [S74][S46]. A belt of glass bottles donated by the poor rings the base of each dome [S41][S42].
  - Haji Lane: a narrow lane of shophouses with independent shops and cafes [S45][S46]. STB's precinct map
    points to its pop-art murals [S46] (STB only on the art; murals change, so check before the shoot).
- **Angle for Indonesian readers:**
  - The district was home to Javanese, Sumatran, Baweanese and Banjarese communities [S43][S44].
  - Bussorah Street near the mosque was once "Kampong Kaji" (Pilgrim Village) [S43].
  - Haji Lane hosted Muslim pilgrims on their way to the hajj [S46][S45]. Wikipedia adds that pilgrim-brokers
    there arranged the hajj for Muslims from nearby islands such as Java [S45] (Wikipedia only on Java).
- **Background:** rebuilt and completed in 1932 to a design by Denis Santry of Swan & Maclaren [S41][S42];
  gazetted a national monument in **1975** [S42][S76]. Print the year only: the day conflicts (see Conflicts).
- **Mosque etiquette (official)** [S39]:
  - Walk-in visits Saturday–Thursday, 10am–12pm and 2–4pm. **Closed to visitors on Friday.**
  - Enter by Gate 5, facing Bussorah Street.
  - Dress code: men in sleeved tops and long pants. Women in long-sleeved tops, no cleavage and bottoms to the
    ankle. Nothing see-through. Robes are lent at the counter.
  - Remove footwear. Keep silent.
  - Visitors may enter the prayer halls only with a mosque official.
  - **"Photography without flash is allowed. Approval is required for video recording."**
- **Permit for a pre-wedding shoot at the mosque: not confirmed.** No official page addresses it.
  - Treat the mosque as a **backdrop from the public street**, where the IMDA street rule applies [S9].
  - Do not stage a gown shoot in the mosque compound without written approval.
  - Avoid prayer times; the mosque office is closed during them [S39].
- **Haji Lane permit:** public street, IMDA rule [S9]. Shopfronts belong to businesses; ask before using a shop
  interior or doorway as a set [S9].

### 7. Koon Seng Road (Joo Chiat): pastel Peranakan terrace houses

- **Name and area:** Koon Seng Road, in the Joo Chiat Conservation Area (Katong–Joo Chiat) [S32][S33].
- **MRT:** about 1 km by straight line to **either** Marine Parade (TE26, 934 m) **or** Eunos (EW7, 982 m) [S35].
  - Wikipedia places Marine Parade station at the end of Joo Chiat Road [S69].
  - Do not promise a short walk.
- **The shot:**
  - Two- and three-storey houses with colourful, ornate facades in Peranakan or Chinese styles [S33]; URA calls
    them pre-war terrace houses [S32].
  - Details: bat-shaped vents on the first storey (Chinese, for good luck), timber fretwork fascia boards
    (Malay), and plaster dentils and floral swags (Western) (official, URA only) [S32].
  - URA calls them "some of the finest examples of Straits Eclectic architecture" in the area [S32].
  - The tourism board's own precinct map lists the road with "Pose in front of the prettiest street in
    Singapore" [S68]; TheSmartLocal calls it a hit "with tourists and Instagrammers alike" [S34].
- **Background:** most were built in the 1920s and 1930s and gazetted for conservation in 1991 [S33] (URA:
  "pre-war") [S32]. The road was renamed in 1934 after Cheong Koon Seng [S32].
- **Etiquette:**
  - **These are lived-in homes.** URA describes them as terrace houses with "residential fronts"
    (timber casement windows flanking a double-leafed timber door) [S32].
  - TheSmartLocal: "keep in mind that it's a residential area and to be respectful of the residents' privacy"
    [S34].
  - Official rule: shoot from the public road without blocking it. Property use needs the owner's approval.
    Neighbours should be told as a courtesy [S9].
  - Do not climb on steps or five-foot ways, lean on gates or open doors.
- **Permit:** none needed on the public street if traffic is not obstructed (official) [S9]. Fee: none
  (no permit).

### 8. Emerald Hill: Peranakan terraces a block off Orchard Road

- **Name and area:** Emerald Hill Conservation Area, off Orchard Road, including Peranakan Place at its mouth
  [S36][S38].
- **MRT:** Somerset (NS23), 417 m [S35]; Dhoby Ghaut, 934 m [S35].
- **The shot:**
  - Terrace houses in Transitional to Art-Deco styles [S36].
  - Many have ornate *pintu pagar* [S36][S37], which Roots describes as "fence-like pairs of revolving doors"
    [S37], and ground-floor facades decorated with ceramic tilework [S36] (Roots: "colourful wall tiles") [S37].
  - Plaster and wood carving produce what URA calls "Chinese Baroque" [S36].
- **Background:**
  - Built up from about 1901 for wealthy Chinese and Straits Chinese families [S36][S38].
  - By the 1930s many houses were occupied by Peranakan families [S37] (Roots only; URA says Chinese and
    Straits-Chinese families [S36]).
  - URA announced it as Singapore's **first** conservation area in 1985 [S36]; it was gazetted in 1989 [S37].
- **Etiquette:** URA says the area "today is an attractive and quiet residential area", with commercial units
  only near Orchard Road [S36]. The same IMDA courtesy rules as Koon Seng Road apply [S9].
- **Permit:** public street, IMDA rule [S9].
- **Why it's less saturated than Koon Seng:** not sourced. Do not claim crowd levels.

### 9. Tiong Bahru estate: 1930s Streamline Moderne flats

- **Name and area:** Tiong Bahru estate, Singapore Improvement Trust (SIT) flats around Seng Poh Road and Moh
  Guan Terrace [S47][S48].
- **MRT:** **Havelock (TE16)** is 440 m from Tiong Bahru Market, closer than Tiong Bahru (EW17) at 597 m [S35].
  Wikipedia confirms both serve the estate and calls Havelock "more accessible from some parts" [S48].
- **The shot:**
  - Low-rise flats in Streamline Moderne, a late development of Art Deco [S47][S48]. Roots notes the curved
    corners and cantilevered shades of the Tiong Bahru blocks [S47]; Wikipedia, "curved horizontal lines" [S48].
    Roots lists porthole windows and flat roofs as features of the style in general, not of these blocks
    specifically [S47], so do not promise portholes.
  - Patterned fair-faced brick balconies can be seen at blocks 57, 82 and 76 [S47] (single source on block
    numbers).
- **Background:** SIT built Singapore's first public housing estate here [S48][S72]; architect Alfred G. Church
  designed the flats between 1936 and 1941. In 2003, URA gazetted 20 pre-war blocks for conservation [S47][S48].
- **Etiquette:** the blocks are homes [S47][S48]. IMDA notes that common areas of HDB estates (corridors, lift
  lobbies, playgrounds) are under Town Council control [S9]. Shoot facades from public paths. Do not shoot in
  corridors or stairwells without the managing body's consent.
  - Whether the conserved SIT blocks are HDB/Town Council or privately managed today: **not confirmed**.
- **Permit:** public street, IMDA rule [S9].

### 10. Singapore Botanic Gardens: the Bandstand and Swan Lake

- **Name and area:** Singapore Botanic Gardens, "Singapore's first and only UNESCO World Heritage Site"
  (official) [S3]. Wikipedia adds that it is the only tropical garden on the list [S67] (Wikipedia only; do not
  print that part).
- **MRT:** Botanic Gardens (CC19/DT9) for the Bukit Timah entrance; Napier (TE12) for the Tanglin entrance
  [S5][S67].
- **The shot:**
  - The **Bandstand**, a historic gazebo [S67] (Wikipedia only for the description; photo candidates P24 show
    it). The Gardens' own page confirms it as a solemnisation venue [S3].
  - **Swan Lake**, excavated in 1866 [S67] (Wikipedia only for the date). One Commons photo shows the curved branches of a roughly
    150-year-old Ficus reflected in it (photo caption only; do not print the age).
  - **Burkill Hall**, a colonial plantation bungalow [S67] (Wikipedia only; also an indoor rental venue per the
    Gardens' "Rent an Indoor Venue" listing [S3]).
- **Fresh angle:** UNESCO heritage plus colonial bungalow without Marina Bay crowds. The Bandstand is also a
  solemnisation venue [S3].
  - **Note: the Bandstand is unavailable for solemnisations from 10 Nov 2026 to 10 Jan 2027** (official) [S3].
- **Permit (official):**
  - Casual sessions need no submission; filming and photography permits need at least 14 days [S3].
  - Excluded from NParks' small-scale permit [S1].
  - Under the Gardens' permit terms: only a hand-held camera, a tripod, boom mic, monitor and hand-held
    reflectors [S4].
  - No props, backgrounds or decoration without approval [S4].
  - No shooting at the 7 major entrance areas and their surroundings (including Palm Court), in the Rainforest,
    Mingxin Foundation Rambler's Ridge, OCBC Arboretum, COMO Adventure Grove or Jacob Ballas Children's Garden,
    at the Heritage Museum and galleries, at the Shaw Foundation Symphony Stage, in office buildings, car parks,
    shops and F&B outlets, **or in "Shelters and all sheltered areas within the Gardens"** unless NParks
    approves [S4].
  - No scenes "posing with swimsuit" or on a bicycle [S4].
  - **Whether the Bandstand counts as a sheltered area: not confirmed. Ask NParks.**
  - No drones; no business without approval [S3].
  - Fee: none stated.
- **Hours (official):** 5am–midnight daily; free entry except the National Orchid Garden [S5][S67].

### 11. Marina Barrage: the skyline from the other side

- **Name and area:** Marina Barrage, 8 Marina Gardens Drive [S35], with a public Green Roof [S56][S58].
- **MRT:** Gardens by the Bay (TE22), 370 m [S57][S35]. SBS bus 400 also runs there [S57].
- **The shot:** a sloping grass roof with "sweeping views of the Singapore skyline" across Marina Reservoir. It
  is used for kite-flying and picnics [S56][S59]. This is the alternative to the standard Marina Bay Sands
  frame.
- **Hours (official):** public areas and the Green Roof are open 24 hours [S57]. Wikipedia also says
  "open for viewing 24/7" [S58].
- **Permit (official):**
  - Commercial filming and photography need PUB approval [S54].
  - **PUB's form allows filming only between 7am and 7pm.** Night skyline shots may not be approved under a
    commercial permit [S55].
  - Drones need a CAAS permit first [S54].
  - Fee: not stated.
  - Whether a pre-wedding shoot is "commercial": **not confirmed**.

### 12. Henderson Waves: wooden wave bridge lit at night

- **Name and area:** Henderson Waves, in Telok Blangah Hill Park on the Southern Ridges, crossing Henderson Road
  [S49][S50].
- **MRT:** Telok Blangah (CC28), 868 m straight-line; it is an uphill walk [S35].
  - NParks' Southern Ridges walk starts from HarbourFront (NE1/CC29) via Mount Faber [S53].
- **The shot:**
  - A 274 m wood-decked bridge with seven undulating "ribs" that form sheltered alcoves [S49][S50].
  - Singapore's tallest pedestrian bridge [S49][S50]. (The height, 36 m above Henderson Road, is Wikipedia only
    [S50]; do not print the figure.)
  - **At night it is lit with LEDs from 7pm to 2am** [S49][S51].
- **Fresh angle:** a blue-hour and night shoot, which few couples plan. NParks itself suggests evenings to see
  it lit [S49].
- **Permit (official):**
  - Telok Blangah Hill Park is **not** on the small-scale exclusion list, so the small-scale NParks permit
    applies to commercial shoots [S1].
  - At least 1 month ahead; no fee; no flash, props or confetti; tripod only [S1].
  - Whether a pre-wedding shoot needs it: **not confirmed** (see framing problem).
- **Hours (official):** park open 24 hours, except do not enter the Earth Trail after 7pm [S52].

### 13. Pulau Ubin: the last kampong village and Chek Jawa boardwalk

- **Name and area:** Pulau Ubin, an island off the north-eastern corner of mainland Singapore [S60][S62]; Chek
  Jawa Wetlands at its eastern end [S71][S73] (Wikipedia: "south-eastern tip") [S63].
- **Getting there (no MRT on the island)** [S60]:
  - Take a bumboat from Changi Point Ferry Terminal, 51 Lorong Bekukong.
  - To reach the terminal: bus 59 from Tampines East (DT33), bus 29 from Tampines (EW2/DT32), or bus 2 from
    Tanah Merah (EW4).
  - No station is within walking distance of the terminal. Tampines East, the nearest station on NParks' route,
    is 5.3 km straight-line; Changi Airport (CG2) is closer at about 3.7 km but NParks does not route through it
    [S35][S60].
  - Bumboats are privately run on demand, about 6am–7pm, leaving when 12 passengers are aboard. The 10-minute
    trip "typically costs $4 per person each way, with an additional $2 for bicycles". Trips outside those hours
    or with fewer passengers cost more, and NParks says "Do confirm fares before each departure" (official
    NParks wording; operator fares can vary) [S60].
- **The shot:**
  - Wooden kampong houses and jetties [S62][S73], abandoned granite quarries [S60][S62].
  - Chek Jawa's coastal and mangrove boardwalks, where several ecosystems meet [S63][S71].
- **Current closures (official)** [S61]:
  - A section of Chek Jawa's Coastal Loop boardwalk is closed for maintenance from 15 Sep 2026; expected
    completion 30 Apr 2027. The Mangrove Loop and the rest of the Coastal Loop stay open.
  - The Chek Jawa Wetlands Tour is suspended until further notice, and a separate track closure from Chek Jawa
    is in force "until further notice".
  - Chek Jawa (boardwalks, Jejawi Tower, visitor centre) is open 7am–7pm daily.
- **Permit (official):**
  - NParks on-location permit [S1].
  - Shoots in nature reserves and nature parks are reviewed "stringently" [S1].
  - Whether Pulau Ubin or Chek Jawa falls in that category for permit purposes: **not confirmed**.
  - Practical line: apply early and expect questions.
- **Etiquette:** some villagers still live on the island (about 40 as of 2012, per Wikipedia only) [S62]. Ask
  before photographing anyone's house.

### 14. Tanjong Beach, Sentosa: the quiet beach at sunset

- **Name and area:** Tanjong Beach, on "the relatively more secluded eastern part" of Sentosa's southern coast
  [S66] (Wikipedia only for "eastern"; Sentosa's own page does not give a position, so write "on Sentosa").
- **MRT:** HarbourFront (NE1/CC29). From VivoCity take the Sentosa Express to Beach Station, then the Beach
  Shuttle to Tanjong Beach [S65][S66]. (Wikipedia calls the shuttle "free-to-ride" [S66]; Sentosa's Tanjong Beach
  page does not mention a fare, so do not print "free".)
- **The shot:** Sentosa describes it as "a favourite with guests who treasure its tranquility and solitude",
  "especially romantic by night, with the twinkling lights of nearby ships" [S65]. This is the operator's own
  description, so attribute it.
  - A public art installation, *Lobster Beach by Philip Colbert*, is currently at the beach [S65]. Its dates are
    not confirmed, so check before the shoot.
- **Permit (official):**
  - Contact SDC at least 10 working days ahead for filming or photography requests (communications@sentosa.gov.sg)
    [S64].
  - Recreational drones are banned on Sentosa [S64].
  - Fee: **not confirmed**. Whether personal shoots need approval: **not confirmed**.
